# Ensemble: Michel Camilo / Steve Khan orbit

## Context

Michel Camilo is a Dominican pianist whose style fuses Latin rhythms, bebop harmony, and virtuosic classical technique. Steve Khan built his reputation through the Eyewitness band and studio session work in New York, anchored by a core rhythm section of Anthony Jackson and Steve Gadd. Together these players represent a meeting point of jazz, Latin, and post-bop fusion from the late 1970s through the 2000s.

## Anthony Jackson

Anthony Jackson invented the six-string contrabass guitar, commissioning the first instrument from luthier Ken Parker in 1975 and coining the term "contrabass guitar." He was a prolific New York session bassist before developing that instrument, playing on O'Jays hits and early Steely Dan sessions, then became central to Steve Khan's Eyewitness group and Michel Camilo's recordings throughout the 1990s.

## Steve Khan

Steve Khan is a New York-based jazz guitarist, son of lyricist Sammy Cahn, who built a reputation through 1970s session work (Steely Dan, Larry Carlton sessions) before forming the Eyewitness band with Anthony Jackson, Steve Jordan, and Manolo Badrena in the early 1980s. His playing draws on post-bop harmony, jazz chord-melody tradition, and interactive improvisation.

## Steve Gadd

Steve Gadd is one of the most recorded drummers in history, known for the iconic groove on Paul Simon's "50 Ways to Leave Your Lover" (1975) and the "Aja" pocket with Steely Dan (1977), both of which became definitive study pieces for generations of drummers. He brought that same combination of swing, precision, and groove intelligence to Michel Camilo's recordings in the early-to-mid 1990s.

## Michel Camilo

Michel Camilo (born 1954) is a Dominican pianist and composer who studied at the National Conservatory in Santo Domingo and later at Juilliard and Mannes in New York. His style fuses bebop harmony, Latin rhythmic vocabulary (particularly Caribbean and Afro-Cuban feels), and a classically trained technique that produces an unusually percussive, driving piano sound.

## Grover Washington Jr.

Grover Washington Jr. (1943–1999) was a Philadelphia-based saxophonist who became the central figure in smooth jazz and soul-jazz through his work on CTI Records in the early 1970s and his Grammy-winning *Winelight* album (1980). He played soprano, alto, tenor, and soprano saxophone and was known for a warm, accessible tone that bridged jazz improvisation and R&B song structures.
