# Ensemble: electric fusion circuit

## Context

UZEB was a Canadian jazz-fusion trio formed in Montreal in 1976, built around bassist Alain Caron's extraordinary fretless 6-string technique and the compositional ambitions of guitarist Michel Cusson and drummer Paul Brochu. Their recordings are among the most technically demanding in the electric fusion canon — Caron's fretless lines are often as melodically active as a lead instrument. Mike Stern and Frank Gambale each developed their own high-level fusion vocabularies in the American jazz context, and all three guitarists share a common language of bebop-influenced phrasing combined with rock-derived technique and electric tone.

## Alain Caron

Alain Caron is a bassist associated with jazz fusion and electric jazz. His playing on a fretless 6-string bass — with extraordinary intonation, a singing melodic voice, and rhythmic fluency across complex meters — made him one of the defining electric bassists of the 1980s and 1990s. He co-led UZEB and has released solo albums continuing in the same high-level fusion direction.

## Mike Stern

Mike Stern is a guitarist associated with jazz fusion and hard bop. He came to prominence through Miles Davis's early-1980s electric band and has led his own projects since, recording consistently for Atlantic Jazz. His playing combines bebop harmonic fluency with a distorted rock tone — a combination that places him in the same zone as John Scofield but with a more aggressive edge.

## Frank Gambale

Frank Gambale is a guitarist associated with jazz fusion. He developed the "sweep picking" technique to a systematic degree — his book and instructional videos on economy picking became widely used teaching tools in the guitar community. He has been a long-standing member of Chick Corea's Elektric Band and has recorded many solo albums for Legato Records.

## Paul Brochu

Paul Brochu is a drummer associated with jazz fusion. He was the drummer for UZEB across the band's entire existence, developing an interactive style that served the band's demanding compositional structures. His playing combined jazz ride-cymbal language with more aggressive rock-derived energy, supporting Caron's melodic bass lines without over-filling.
