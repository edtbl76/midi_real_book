# Ensemble: Ozzy Osbourne

## Context

Ozzy Osbourne launched his solo career in 1980 following his departure from Black Sabbath, building a band around a rotating cast of lead guitarists who each defined a distinct era: Randy Rhoads brought neoclassical precision to the first two records, Jake E. Lee drove the glam-metal mid-period, and Zakk Wylde anchored the blues-metal decades that followed. What holds the ensemble together across all lineups is Ozzy's melodic vocal phrasing — direct, anthemic, and designed to sit above a dense mix — paired with the guitarist-as-lead-voice format where the guitar carries the weight of both riff and melodic statement. A locked-in heavy rhythm section (bass and drums) provides the foundation that lets each guitarist's personality dominate.

## Robert Trujillo

Robert Trujillo is a Los Angeles-born bassist who built his reputation in the funk-metal band Suicidal Tendencies before joining Ozzy Osbourne's band in the mid-1990s, where he appeared on the live album "Retirement Sucks." He is known for a physical, low-slung fingerstyle technique that combines heavy groove-metal drive with funk-influenced articulation and rhythmic endurance. Trujillo joined Metallica in 2003 and has been their bassist ever since, becoming one of the most prominent figures in hard rock bass playing.

## Zakk Wylde

Zakk Wylde (born Jeffrey Philip Wielandt in 1967 in New Jersey) joined Ozzy Osbourne in 1987 at age 19 and became the dominant voice on every Ozzy record from "No Rest for the Wicked" (1988) through "Black Rain" (2007), with gaps during which he was replaced before returning. He is instantly recognizable for his bull's-eye and camo-finish Les Pauls, his aggressive use of pinch harmonics, and a wide-interval pentatonic vocabulary driven by explosive pick attack and sustained vibrato. Outside of Ozzy, he founded Black Label Society in 1998, a band that allowed him to explore heavier and more personal material as a frontman. He briefly left the Ozzy camp around 2009–2010 when Gus G. was brought in, but returned for subsequent Ozzy projects including the Grammy-winning "Patient Number 9" (2022).

## Jake E. Lee

Jake E. Lee (born Jakey Lou Williams in 1957) replaced Randy Rhoads' replacement Brad Gillis and served as Ozzy Osbourne's guitarist from 1983 to 1987, recording "Bark at the Moon" (1983) and "The Ultimate Sin" (1986). His playing combined aggressive wide-interval riffing, whammy bar theatrics, and a melodic sensibility that fit the glossier production of the mid-1980s metal mainstream. After leaving Ozzy's camp — reportedly under difficult circumstances — he largely retreated from the music industry before returning with his band Badlands in the late 1980s and early 1990s, a blues-metal outfit that earned critical respect. He has remained largely out of the spotlight since Badlands disbanded, though he has occasionally toured and recorded.

## Mike Bordin

Mike Bordin is an Oakland-born drummer best known as the founding drummer and consistent backbone of Faith No More since their formation in 1983. He joined Ozzy Osbourne's touring and recording band in the mid-1990s, appearing on "Ozzmosis" (1995) and "Down to Earth" (2001) as well as extensive live work. Bordin is known for a thunderous, tribal-influenced playing style that combines powerful kick and snare attack with dynamic sensitivity, and he is recognizable by his dreadlocked appearance and intense physical presence behind the kit. His parallel career with Faith No More — including records like "The Real Thing" (1989) and "Angel Dust" (1992) — established him as one of alternative metal's defining drummers.

## Bob Daisley

Bob Daisley is an Australian bassist who played a foundational role in Ozzy Osbourne's solo career, appearing on "Blizzard of Ozz" (1980) and "Diary of a Madman" (1981) alongside Randy Rhoads, and later on "Bark at the Moon" (1983) and "No More Tears" (1991) among others. Beyond the bass parts, Daisley co-wrote much of the lyrical and compositional content of those early Ozzy albums, making him one of the most significant behind-the-scenes architects of the Osbourne solo catalog. Before Ozzy, he had worked with Rainbow and Uriah Heep, and he has remained active across hard rock and metal projects throughout his career.

## Randy Rhoads

Randy Rhoads (1956–1982) was a California-born guitarist who co-founded the glam-metal band Quiet Riot before joining Ozzy Osbourne in 1979, recording "Blizzard of Ozz" (1980) and "Diary of a Madman" (1981) — two albums that defined the neoclassical metal genre. His playing fused formal classical guitar training with aggressive metal technique: precise alternate picking, sweep arpeggios, diminished-scale runs, and a compositional approach to soloing that placed him in a different category from most of his contemporaries. He was killed on March 19, 1982, at age 25, in a plane crash during a tour stop in Florida, cutting short a career that had already placed him among the most influential guitarists in rock history. His influence on subsequent generations of lead guitarists — particularly those operating in the neoclassical and shred traditions — remains immeasurable.

## Gary Moore

Gary Moore (1952–2011) was a Belfast-born guitarist who briefly joined Ozzy Osbourne's band in 1979 as a touring guitarist, before Randy Rhoads was recruited to record the debut album — making Moore's time with Ozzy significant but very short-lived. He is far better known for his own solo career, which traced an arc from hard rock and heavy metal in the 1970s and 1980s — including acclaimed albums with Thin Lizzy and his solo records "Corridors of Power" and "Victims of the Future" — to emotionally intense blues-rock on "Still Got the Blues" (1990), which brought him to a much wider audience. Moore's playing was defined by extraordinary vibrato, raw emotional intensity, and a mastery of tone that made individual notes carry dramatic weight.

## Ritchie Blackmore

Ritchie Blackmore is an English guitarist who co-founded Deep Purple in 1968 and led the band through its most commercially and artistically successful periods, including the "Mark II" lineup that recorded "Machine Head" (1972) and "Burn" (1974). After leaving Deep Purple in 1975, he formed Rainbow, a band that moved closer to classical European melodic structures and paved much of the conceptual ground that Randy Rhoads and the neoclassical metal movement would later develop. Blackmore's influence on the Ozzy Osbourne ensemble is substantial but indirect — his baroque-influenced alternate picking, modal awareness, and treatment of the guitar as a classical voice were formative on Randy Rhoads. He returned to Deep Purple in 1984, then departed again in 1993 to form Blackmore's Night, a Renaissance-influenced acoustic project with his partner Candice Night.

## Uli Jon Roth

Uli Jon Roth is a German guitarist who was the lead guitarist for Scorpions from 1973 to 1978, recording a series of albums — including "In Trance" (1975), "Virgin Killer" (1976), and "Tokyo Tapes" (1978) — that documented his evolution from psychedelic rock toward a proto-neoclassical vocabulary that prefigured the shred movement by nearly a decade. His advanced use of vibrato, chromatic scale runs, and classical phrasing approaches were widely influential on the generation of guitarists that included Randy Rhoads. After leaving Scorpions, Roth pursued an increasingly classical direction through his Electric Sun project and later solo career, developing his own "Sky Guitar" instruments designed to extend the range and tonal palette of the electric guitar.

## Tommy Aldridge

Tommy Aldridge is a Mississippi-born drummer who joined Ozzy Osbourne's band in 1981, touring extensively during the Diary of a Madman era following Lee Kerslake's departure, and then recording "Bark at the Moon" (1983) and "The Ultimate Sin" (1986). He is known for a thunderous, technically sophisticated double-bass style and an approach to fills that favors powerful, deliberate strokes over speed-for-its-own-sake — traits that suited the heavy, dramatic requirements of Ozzy's mid-1980s band. Aldridge is also recognized for his bare-handed drumming technique, in which he finishes certain solos with his palms rather than sticks. Prior to Ozzy, he worked with Pat Travers and Black Oak Arkansas, and after leaving the Ozzy camp he joined Whitesnake and later Thin Lizzy touring lineups.

## Don Airey

Don Airey is an English keyboardist who played on Ozzy Osbourne's "Blizzard of Ozz" (1980) and "Diary of a Madman" (1981), providing the orchestral and gothic keyboard textures that helped define the neoclassical atmosphere of those records alongside Randy Rhoads. He is a busy session player and touring musician who has worked across a wide swath of British hard rock and metal, including Rainbow, Whitesnake, Gary Moore's band, and Colosseum II. Since 2002, Airey has been the full-time keyboardist for Deep Purple, contributing to the band's most recent studio albums and extensive touring. His keyboard work on the early Ozzy albums — the ominous synth lines, the classical-leaning flourishes — is a key but often underappreciated part of what made those records feel so dramatically different from the heavy metal of the time.

## Rudy Sarzo

Rudy Sarzo is a Cuban-born bassist who joined Ozzy Osbourne's touring band in 1981 for the Diary of a Madman tour, playing alongside Randy Rhoads in what became one of the most celebrated live lineups in heavy metal history. He was present at the tour stop in Florida in March 1982 when Randy Rhoads was killed in a plane crash — a moment that ended an era before it had fully registered. After leaving Ozzy's band, Sarzo joined Quiet Riot, appearing on "Metal Health" (1983), the first heavy metal album to debut at number one on the Billboard 200. He subsequently played with Whitesnake, Ozzy again briefly, Dio, and Queensryche, establishing himself as one of the most recognized sideman bassists in classic metal.
