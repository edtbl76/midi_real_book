# Ensemble: Tool

## Context

Tool formed in Los Angeles in 1990 and became one of the defining acts in progressive metal and art metal, known for long albums, extended song structures, and decade-long gaps between releases — their five studio albums span 1993 to 2019. What sets the ensemble apart technically is Danny Carey's polyrhythmic drumming, widely considered the most complex in mainstream rock, combined with Adam Jones's slow-building, sustain-heavy guitar textures, Justin Chancellor's melodic and harmonically active bass lines, and Maynard James Keenan's oblique, often cryptic lyrics delivered with precise vocal placement that treats the voice as another instrument inside the arrangement rather than above it.

## Justin Chancellor

Justin Chancellor was born in London in 1971 and grew up playing bass in the UK before relocating to Los Angeles in the early 1990s, where he replaced original Tool bassist Paul D'Amour in 1995. He contributed to every album from Aenima (1996) onward, developing a signature approach built on the Wal Mk2 bass — a thick, harmonically complex instrument that sits between the guitar and drums rather than simply doubling one or the other. His lines are melodic but rhythmically precise, often weaving polyrhythmic figures with Carey while maintaining forward momentum through odd meters that might otherwise feel unstable. On tracks like Schism and The Pot he demonstrates how a bass part can carry harmonic interest, rhythmic counterpoint, and groove simultaneously without overplaying.

## Adam Jones

Adam Jones was born in Libertyville, Illinois in 1965 and trained as a special effects artist and sculptor, working on films including Jurassic Park and Terminator 2 before committing fully to Tool following the band's early success. He was a founding member of Tool in 1990 and has remained the band's sole guitarist throughout their career, developing a sound centered on slow-moving, sustained riff architecture built from alternate tunings and long-decay tones rather than speed or flash. Jones is the primary director behind Tool's pioneering stop-motion music videos, which he has designed and directed for the band since the early 1990s. His playing is characterized by heavy muting, deliberate picking attack, and the way riffs function as rhythmic events as much as melodic ones — particularly effective in odd-meter contexts where the guitar's placement within the bar shifts across repetitions.

## Danny Carey

Danny Carey was born in Lawrence, Kansas in 1961 and studied music formally before moving to Los Angeles in the mid-1980s, where he played in the studio scene and with numerous acts before co-founding Tool in 1990. He is widely regarded as one of the most technically advanced drummers in rock music, with a playing style informed by mathematics, sacred geometry, jazz, and progressive rock — in particular the influence of Bill Bruford and Elvin Jones is audible in his use of polyrhythm and his approach to the kit as a melodic-harmonic instrument, not just a timekeeping device. Carey has long used electronic triggers and hand percussion alongside his acoustic kit, and his kit setups have expanded across Tool's career to include an extensive array of Paiste cymbals, Tama hardware, and custom-built instruments. His most celebrated technique is the ability to play in two or more simultaneous meters across limbs — a practice directly audible in Schism, Lateralus, and much of the Fear Inoculum record — without the composite rhythm losing forward momentum or feel.

## Maynard James Keenan

Maynard James Keenan was born in Ravenna, Ohio in 1964 and served in the U.S. Army before studying art and design in Boston, relocating to Los Angeles in the late 1980s where he began performing in local bands before co-founding Tool in 1990. His vocal approach is less about conventional rock delivery and more about placement — he uses space, register shifts, and extended silence between phrases as compositional elements, and his lyrics tend toward psychological, philosophical, and mythological themes rather than direct narrative. Keenan is one of rock's most active side-project artists: A Perfect Circle, begun in 1999 with guitarist Billy Howerdel, operates as a separate primary band rather than a peripheral project and has released four studio albums; Puscifer, begun around 2003, is a more experimental and deliberately eclectic project with a rotating cast of collaborators and a touring production that incorporates theatrical staging. He also co-owns Caduceus Cellars and Merkin Vineyards in Arizona, which are serious winemaking operations, not hobby projects. His stage presence with Tool is famously minimal — he typically performs from the back of the stage or in near-darkness, redirecting visual attention toward the other musicians.
