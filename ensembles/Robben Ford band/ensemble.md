# Ensemble: Robben Ford band

## Context

Robben Ford's band operates at the intersection of Texas blues, jazz-influenced rock, and blues-fusion. Ford built his reputation through stints with the L.A. Express and as a Yellowjackets co-founder before focusing on his solo career, where the Blue Line lineup — with Roscoe Beck on bass and Tom Brechtlein on drums — became his most celebrated working band. The ensemble plays with a refined pocket: unhurried grooves, sophisticated chord voicings, and Ford's distinctively warm, Dumble-amplified guitar tone.

## Roscoe Beck

Roscoe Beck is a Dallas-based bassist who served as Robben Ford's primary bass collaborator across the Blue Line years and beyond. He is also long-running touring bassist for Eric Johnson, and co-designed the Fender Roscoe Beck Signature bass. His playing is notable for deep pocket, warm tone, and the ability to support sophisticated chord changes without overplaying.

## Robben Ford

Robben Ford (b. 1951) is a blues-jazz guitarist who came up through the L.A. Express (with Tom Scott, 1972–1976) before co-founding the Yellowjackets in 1977. After leaving the Yellowjackets in 1983, he pursued a solo career that fused Texas blues vocabulary with jazz harmony and chord-melody sophistication. His tone — built around a vintage Gibson ES-335 and a Dumble amplifier — is one of the most immediately recognizable sounds in blues-jazz guitar.

## Tom Brechtlein

Tom Brechtlein is Robben Ford's long-running drummer, present across the Blue Line era and the albums that followed. He also recorded with Chick Corea's Elektric Band. His style in Ford's context is groove-first: he keeps the blues pocket deep and natural while providing the dynamic control that lets the ensemble breathe on ballads and push on up-tempo shuffles.

## Reese Wynans

Reese Wynans (b. 1947) was the keyboardist for Stevie Ray Vaughan & Double Trouble from 1985 until SRV's death in 1990, appearing on Soul to Soul (1985), Live Alive (1986), and In Step (1989). He operates in the same Texas blues-rock world as Robben Ford, bringing Hammond B-3 gospel weight and Fender Rhodes warmth to blues and blues-rock arrangements. His role is harmonic color and ensemble glue rather than soloist-forward playing.
