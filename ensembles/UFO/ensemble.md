# Ensemble: UFO

## Context

UFO formed in London in 1969 and spent their early years as a space rock outfit before the arrival of 19-year-old Michael Schenker from the Scorpions in 1973 transformed them into one of British hard rock's most distinctive bands. The albums Phenomenon (1974) through Obsession (1978) represent their peak: Schenker's melodic precision and natural-minor command sit above Pete Way's fluid, singing bass lines and Phil Mogg's blue-collar lyrical directness, all anchored by Andy Parker's driving, uncluttered backbeat. That interplay — melodic leads, vocal bass movement, and direct rhythmic authority — is the sonic identity that makes these records essential hard rock listening.

## Michael Schenker

Michael Schenker was born in Sarstedt, Germany in 1955 and joined UFO from the Scorpions in 1973 at age 17, immediately redefining the band's direction toward melodic hard rock. His style is built on natural minor and harmonic minor command, singing vibrato, and a sense of melodic architecture that gives solos genuine thematic development rather than speed for its own sake. The Gibson Flying V — particularly his iconic silver-and-black "Silver Bullet" — became inseparable from his sound, run through Marshall Super Lead 100W heads for a singing, compressed high-gain tone that remains one of hard rock's most recognizable guitar sounds. His work on Phenomenon, Force It, No Heavy Petting, Lights Out, and Obsession is the blueprint for melody-first hard rock lead playing, and the live document Strangers in the Night (1979) captures that peak at concert volume. He left UFO in 1979 and went on to lead the Michael Schenker Group, but the UFO catalog remains his most celebrated body of work.

## Cliff Williams

Cliff Williams is a bassist best known as a long-serving member of AC/DC, which he joined in 1977 after stints with several British rock bands including Home and Laurie. His playing style is rooted in economy and consistency — lock-in-with-the-drummer hard rock bass that prioritizes feel and reliability over complexity. While Williams is not a UFO member, his approach shares the same British hard rock economy that defines Pete Way's foundation work, making his playing a useful comparative reference for understanding how bassists support riff-driven rock. In AC/DC he formed one of hard rock's most studied rhythm sections with Phil Rudd, playing Fender basses through Marshall amplification and remaining one of the most consistent low-end anchors in rock for over four decades.

## Phil Rudd

Phil Rudd is the drummer of AC/DC and one of the most studied practitioners of rhythmic minimalism in hard rock. His playing philosophy is built entirely on feel, consistency, and groove — he plays less than almost any other drummer at his level of influence, and the result is a time-feel so solid that it became a foundational reference for hard rock drumming. Rudd's hi-hat work is particularly distinctive: a locked, slightly behind-the-beat lilt that gives AC/DC's rhythm section its unmistakable swagger. He has played predominantly on Ludwig and DW kits with Zildjian cymbals throughout his career. Like Williams, Perry, and Angus Young, Rudd is not a UFO member, but his approach to drumming in riff-based hard rock offers a clear parallel to Andy Parker's work and is an essential comparative study.
