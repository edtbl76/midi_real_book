# Ensemble: John Mayer Trio

## Context

The John Mayer Trio formed in 2005 around the live album *Try!* (2005), with Mayer also drawing on the lineup for tracks on *Battle Studies* (2009). It is a blues-rock power trio — John Mayer on guitar, Steve Jordan on drums, and Pino Palladino on bass — distinguished from Mayer's pop work by its heavier blues-rock vocabulary, longer improvisational passages, and an explicit debt to Stevie Ray Vaughan and Cream. The format strips away keyboards and backing vocals, putting the full weight on guitar tone, ensemble interplay, and dynamic control across a three-piece.

## Pino Palladino

Pino Palladino is a Welsh bassist born in Cardiff who built his reputation in the early 1980s through session work in the UK, most notably on Paul Young's "Wherever I Lay My Head" and Don Henley's "Boys of Summer," where his fretless bass playing became one of the most recognized sounds of the decade. He transitioned to fretted bass in the 1990s, broadening his work into harder rock and R&B contexts while losing none of his melodic sensibility. His profile expanded dramatically after appearing on D'Angelo's *Voodoo* (2000), a landmark neo-soul record on which his locked-in rhythmic feel and deep pocket became central to the album's identity. He later became a member of The Who following John Entwistle's death in 2002, and his role in the John Mayer Trio placed him in an explicitly blues-rock setting that showcased his ability to serve a power trio format without overplaying.

## John Mayer

John Mayer was born in Bridgeport, Connecticut in 1977 and enrolled at Berklee College of Music before dropping out and relocating to Atlanta, where he built an independent following before signing to a major label. His early commercial work — *Room for Squares* (2001), *Heavier Things* (2003) — established him as a pop-oriented singer-songwriter, but his guitar playing consistently pointed toward blues, and the formation of the John Mayer Trio in 2005 made that influence explicit. He has won multiple Grammy Awards across both pop and blues categories, including Best Male Pop Vocal Performance and Best Traditional Blues Album (for *At the Crossroads*). His later career has moved increasingly toward the blues-rock and Americana end of the spectrum, including his tenure with Dead & Company alongside members of the Grateful Dead from 2015 onward.

## Steve Jordan

Steve Jordan is a New York-born drummer and producer whose session résumé spans four decades of American popular music. He became a first-call New York session drummer in the late 1970s and 1980s, appearing on records for Steely Dan, James Taylor, and David Sanborn, and served as the house drummer on *Late Night with David Letterman*. His most significant long-term musical relationship has been with Keith Richards and the Rolling Stones organization — he co-produced and performed on Richards's solo albums, including *Talk Is Cheap* (1988), and later became the Stones' drummer following Charlie Watts's death in 2021. He had a personal and musical connection to Stevie Ray Vaughan, which informs both his blues-rock instincts and his approach to the Trio's material. In the Trio context, Jordan's playing is characterized by a deep, deliberate pocket, vintage-drum tone, and an economy that serves the three-piece format without overplaying.

## Mateus Asato

Mateus Asato is a Brazilian guitarist born in São Paulo who built a global following through Instagram and YouTube before any traditional industry pathway — his short-form videos of melodic improvisations became widely circulated in guitar circles from around 2015 onward. He relocated to Los Angeles and became a first-call session guitarist, working with artists including Tori Kelly, before releasing the EP *21/22* (2019). His playing is characterized by lyrical melodic phrasing, sophisticated harmonic choices, and a clean Stratocaster tone that places him directly in the lineage of John Mayer, Larry Carlton, and Robben Ford. His inclusion here reflects the direct stylistic descent from Mayer's blues-rock vocabulary to Asato's melodic pop-guitar approach.

## Questlove

Ahmir Khalib Thompson — known as Questlove — was born in Philadelphia on January 20, 1971, into a musical household: his father was Lee Andrews, a doo-wop artist. He co-founded The Roots in 1987, which became the most critically respected hip-hop band to sustain an acoustic, live-instrument approach; his drumming, rooted in James Brown's JBs and Philadelphia soul, became the rhythmic identity of everything the group recorded from *Organix* (1993) through the band's ongoing work as the Tonight Show house band (since 2014). Beyond The Roots, Questlove has produced and played on records for D'Angelo, Erykah Badu, Amy Winehouse, and John Legend, and directed the acclaimed documentary *Summer of Soul* (2021, Sundance Grand Jury Prize winner). His inclusion in the John Mayer Trio orbit reflects his core role on D'Angelo's *Voodoo* (2000) — the record that also featured Pino Palladino, Roy Hargrove, and the Soulquarians collective that directly influenced Mayer's blues-soul approach.

## D'Angelo

Michael Eugene Archer — known as D'Angelo — was born February 11, 1974, in Richmond, Virginia, into a Pentecostal family where his grandfather and father were both ministers; he learned piano in that devotional context. His debut *Brown Sugar* (1995) made him the breakout figure of the neo-soul movement, and *Voodoo* (2000) expanded his musical vocabulary by working with Pino Palladino, Questlove, Roy Hargrove, and the Soulquarians collective — recording live to tape over eighteen months in a process that became the reference point for a generation trying to recover the analog warmth and rhythmic liveness of 1970s soul. After a long public absence following *Voodoo*'s release, D'Angelo returned with *Black Messiah* (2014), a critically acclaimed record made with a larger band including James Blood Ulmer. His presence in the John Mayer Trio orbit is through *Voodoo* and the musicians who formed that recording's collective — Pino Palladino, Questlove, and Roy Hargrove were D'Angelo's primary creative partners on the record.

## Roy Hargrove

Roy Anthony Hargrove Jr. was born October 16, 1969, in Waco, Texas, and began playing trumpet after a high school teacher arranged for him to meet Wynton Marsalis, giving him direct access to the senior jazz establishment as a teenager. He attended Berklee College of Music and the New School in New York, and by his early twenties was leading his own groups on Verve Records — documenting a bebop-rooted but stylistically wide-ranging body of work including straight-ahead jazz, Latin jazz (with Crisol), and the soul-jazz project the RH Factor. His profile among non-jazz audiences grew through collaborations with D'Angelo (*Voodoo*, 2000), Erykah Badu, Notorious B.I.G., and Common, where he served as the jazz voice bridging hip-hop's rhythmic vocabulary with bebop's melodic precision. Hargrove died in New York City on November 2, 2018, at age forty-nine, from cardiac arrest related to kidney disease.

| [Stardust](https://open.spotify.com/search/Stardust%20Roy%20Hargrove%20Ballads) | The ballad setting that shows the full range of his tonal control; slow tempo, lyrical arc, and the kind of breath-and-phrase shaping that connects trumpet to voice. |
| [On Fire](https://open.spotify.com/search/On%20Fire%20D%27Angelo%20Voodoo%20Roy%20Hargrove) | Hargrove's appearance on the neo-soul landmark that brought him to Pino Palladino and Questlove's collaborative orbit; the trumpet interacts with the groove as a melodic voice rather than a jazz-context soloist. |
| [I'll Stay](https://open.spotify.com/search/I%27ll%20Stay%20RH%20Factor%20Erykah%20Badu) | The RH Factor track featuring Erykah Badu; the soul-jazz ensemble context is fully realized here with Hargrove's trumpet as the lead instrumental voice above a dense rhythm section. |
| [Dienda](https://open.spotify.com/search/Dienda%20Roy%20Hargrove%20Crisol) | The Latin jazz project that showed Hargrove's cross-genre range; his trumpet vocabulary adapts to Afro-Cuban rhythms without losing the bebop core. |
| [The Common Free Style](https://open.spotify.com/search/Common%20Free%20Style%20RH%20Factor%20Hard%20Groove) | Common's guest appearance on the RH Factor album; Hargrove's trumpet in a hip-hop context where the jazz phrasing and the MC cadence alternate rather than compete. |
