# Ensemble: John Mayer Trio

## Context

The John Mayer Trio formed in 2005 around the live album *Try!* (2005), with Mayer also drawing on the lineup for tracks on *Battle Studies* (2009). It is a blues-rock power trio — John Mayer on guitar, Steve Jordan on drums, and Pino Palladino on bass — distinguished from Mayer's pop work by its heavier blues-rock vocabulary, longer improvisational passages, and an explicit debt to Stevie Ray Vaughan and Cream. The format strips away keyboards and backing vocals, putting the full weight on guitar tone, ensemble interplay, and dynamic control across a three-piece.

## Pino Palladino

Pino Palladino is a Welsh bassist born in Cardiff who built his reputation in the early 1980s through session work in the UK, most notably on Paul Young's "Wherever I Lay My Head" and Don Henley's "Boys of Summer," where his fretless bass playing became one of the most recognized sounds of the decade. He transitioned to fretted bass in the 1990s, broadening his work into harder rock and R&B contexts while losing none of his melodic sensibility. His profile expanded dramatically after appearing on D'Angelo's *Voodoo* (2000), a landmark neo-soul record on which his locked-in rhythmic feel and deep pocket became central to the album's identity. He later became a member of The Who following John Entwistle's death in 2002, and his role in the John Mayer Trio placed him in an explicitly blues-rock setting that showcased his ability to serve a power trio format without overplaying.

## John Mayer

John Mayer was born in Bridgeport, Connecticut in 1977 and enrolled at Berklee College of Music before dropping out and relocating to Atlanta, where he built an independent following before signing to a major label. His early commercial work — *Room for Squares* (2001), *Heavier Things* (2003) — established him as a pop-oriented singer-songwriter, but his guitar playing consistently pointed toward blues, and the formation of the John Mayer Trio in 2005 made that influence explicit. He has won multiple Grammy Awards across both pop and blues categories, including Best Male Pop Vocal Performance and Best Traditional Blues Album (for *At the Crossroads*). His later career has moved increasingly toward the blues-rock and Americana end of the spectrum, including his tenure with Dead & Company alongside members of the Grateful Dead from 2015 onward.

## Roy Hargrove

Roy Anthony Hargrove Jr. was born October 16, 1969, in Waco, Texas, and began playing trumpet after a high school teacher arranged for him to meet Wynton Marsalis, giving him direct access to the senior jazz establishment as a teenager. He attended Berklee College of Music and the New School in New York, and by his early twenties was leading his own groups on Verve Records — documenting a bebop-rooted but stylistically wide-ranging body of work including straight-ahead jazz, Latin jazz (with Crisol), and the soul-jazz project the RH Factor. His profile among non-jazz audiences grew through collaborations with D'Angelo (*Voodoo*, 2000), Erykah Badu, Notorious B.I.G., and Common, where he served as the jazz voice bridging hip-hop's rhythmic vocabulary with bebop's melodic precision. Hargrove died in New York City on November 2, 2018, at age forty-nine, from cardiac arrest related to kidney disease.
