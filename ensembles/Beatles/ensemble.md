# Ensemble: Beatles

## Context

The Beatles were active from 1960 to 1970, evolving from Merseybeat-rooted rock and roll — honed through marathon residencies in Hamburg — through the studio-craft sophistication of Revolver and Sgt. Pepper's, and finally into the fragmented but still inventive sessions of the White Album and Abbey Road. What makes their ensemble interplay historically significant is the way four distinct personalities formed a single sound: McCartney's melodic bass lines that function as a second lead voice, Harrison's economic lead playing rooted in Chet Atkins–influenced country picking and later Indian music, Lennon's driving rhythm guitar and harmonic instincts, and Starr's understated but groove-locked drumming — always in service of the song.

## Paul McCartney

Paul McCartney was born on 18 June 1942 in Liverpool. He joined John Lennon's skiffle group the Quarrymen in 1957 and became the Beatles' bassist when the band formed in 1960 — switching from guitar partly because the symmetrical body of the Höfner 500/1 looked less awkward played left-handed. His bass playing evolved from simple root-and-fifth work on early singles into one of the most melodically inventive approaches in rock: by the Revolver era he was treating the bass as a second lead instrument, running counter-melodies against Harrison's guitar and Lennon's vocal. After the Beatles disbanded in 1970 he launched a solo career and formed Wings, the best-selling pop act of the 1970s.

## George Harrison

George Harrison was born on 25 February 1943 in Wavertree, Liverpool. He joined the Quarrymen at the age of fifteen after impressing Lennon with his guitar playing, and became the Beatles' lead guitarist — the youngest member by three years. Known as "the quiet Beatle," Harrison's lead style was shaped early by the country picking of Chet Atkins and Carl Perkins, then deepened through his study of Indian classical music with sitar master Ravi Shankar beginning in 1965. His playing is characteristically melodic and restrained: chromatic voice-leading, purposeful vibrato, and a preference for a complete musical statement over flash. He died of lung cancer on 29 November 2001.

## Ringo Starr

Ringo Starr (born Richard Starkey, 7 July 1940, Dingle, Liverpool) replaced Pete Best as the Beatles' drummer in August 1962, having already established himself as one of the most in-demand drummers on the Merseyside circuit with Rory Storm and the Hurricanes. His playing is defined by a singing tom-tom sound, restrained fill discipline, and an unerring sense of groove: he rarely repeats a fill, places accents where most drummers wouldn't, and consistently prioritises the song over technical display. He converted to Ludwig drums after falling in love with the Oyster Black Pearl finish — the only colour available through Ludwig at the time. As a solo artist he released the critically successful album Ringo (1973), which featured contributions from all three other ex-Beatles.

## Billy Preston

Billy Preston (2 September 1946 – 6 June 2006) was an American keyboardist, singer, and songwriter born in Houston, Texas. A child prodigy who was self-taught, he was playing organ onstage backing gospel singers including Mahalia Jackson by the age of ten. He first met the Beatles in Hamburg in 1962, and was invited to join their troubled Get Back sessions in January 1969 — an addition credited with easing tensions in the studio and galvanising the band's playing. He is the only musician to receive joint billing credit on a Beatles single: "Get Back" (1969) was released as "The Beatles with Billy Preston." He went on to a successful solo career in R&B and funk, earning a Grammy for the instrumental "Outa-Space" (1972).
