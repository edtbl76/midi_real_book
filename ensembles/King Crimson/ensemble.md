# Ensemble: King Crimson

## Context

King Crimson formed in London in 1968 and released *In the Court of the Crimson King* (1969), an album widely regarded as the founding document of progressive rock — the moment the genre's vocabulary of odd meters, orchestral density, and extended improvisation crystallized into a coherent statement. Robert Fripp, the band's sole continuous member, repeatedly dissolved and rebuilt the lineup from scratch rather than simply rotating personnel, producing radically different ensembles across five decades: the orchestral Mk I with Greg Lake and Ian McDonald, the jazz-tinged Mk II with Mel Collins and Boz Burrell, the ferocious Mk III with John Wetton and Bill Bruford, the angular Mk IV with Adrian Belew and Tony Levin, and the double-trio Mk VII that added Pat Mastelotto. Each lineup's interplay is distinct — Mk III leans on asymmetric riffing and free improvisation between Wetton and Bruford, while Mk IV builds interlocking guitar parts from Fripp and Belew over Levin's Chapman Stick — making King Crimson less a single band than a recurring laboratory for ensemble reinvention.

## Tony Levin

Tony Levin (born 1946 in Boston) is an American bassist and Chapman Stick player whose session resume spans John Lennon, Paul Simon, Peter Gabriel, and Pink Floyd, making him one of the most recorded bassists in rock history. He joined King Crimson in 1981 for the Mk IV incarnation after Fripp heard him play a single chorus of "Red" at an audition, and appeared on the Discipline, Beat, and Three of a Perfect Pair albums. Levin returned for the Mk VII "Double Trio" lineup in the mid-1990s, playing alongside a second bassist Trey Gunn and appearing on Thrak (1995). His defining contribution to King Crimson is his Chapman Stick work — the instrument's simultaneous bass and chord registers allow him to fill harmonic space that would normally require two players, while his bass lines in odd meters remain rhythmically authoritative. Outside King Crimson, his longest-running association has been with Peter Gabriel, whose band he has anchored since the early 1980s.

## Adrian Belew

Adrian Belew (born 1949 in Covington, Kentucky) came to international attention when Frank Zappa discovered him performing in a Nashville club in 1977, leading almost immediately to tours with Zappa, David Bowie, and Talking Heads before Robert Fripp recruited him for King Crimson in 1981. In Crimson's Mk IV lineup, Belew served as co-lead guitarist, vocalist, and primary lyricist — his guitar style, characterized by extreme whammy-bar manipulation, animal-sound textures, and percussive rhythmic interlocking with Fripp's guitar, defined the sound of Discipline, Beat, and Three of a Perfect Pair. He returned for the double-trio Mk VII (Thrak, 1995) and the Double Duo that recorded The ConstruKction of Light (2000). Outside Crimson, Belew has led the Adrian Belew Power Trio and released numerous solo albums exploring guitar synthesis, looping, and avant-garde song structures.

## Jordan Rudess

Jordan Rudess (born 1956 in Great Neck, New York) is a classically trained pianist who studied at the Juilliard School before pivoting to progressive rock. He became a member of Dream Theater in 1999, replacing Derek Sherinian, and has remained the band's keyboardist since then. Rudess participated in the first two Liquid Tension Experiment albums (1998, 1999), which is his primary connection to Tony Levin — the two shared a rhythm section with Portnoy and Petrucci. He was never a member of King Crimson. His keyboard approach synthesizes classical technique — fast scalar runs, extended harmonic vocabulary, two-hand independence — with synthesizer sound design and, in recent years, tablet-based performance via the Morphwiz and similar apps he has co-designed.

## Mel Collins

Mel Collins (born 1947 in England) is a multi-woodwind player — tenor and soprano saxophone, flute, and bass flute — who joined King Crimson in 1970 as the replacement for Ian McDonald following the dissolution of the Mk I lineup. He appeared on the Mk II albums *In the Wake of Poseidon* (1970), *Lizard* (1970), *Islands* (1971), and the live *Earthbound* (1972), then left when that lineup dissolved. He subsequently built an extensive session career, playing on records by the Rolling Stones, Dire Straits, Roger Waters, and many others. Collins returned as a guest on one track of *Red* (1974) — the closing "Starless" — reportedly recording his soprano saxophone part in a single take after finishing a Humble Pie session at the same studio. He rejoined King Crimson's later touring lineup in the 2010s as one of several horns in the expanded seven-piece ensemble.
