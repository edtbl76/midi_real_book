# Ensemble: modal jazz reinterpretation

## Context

The John Coltrane Classic Quartet — Coltrane, McCoy Tyner, Jimmy Garrison, and Elvin Jones — is one of the most studied and documented ensembles in jazz history, active between 1960 and 1965 and responsible for recordings from *My Favorite Things* through *A Love Supreme* and *Ascension*. Their approach to modal jazz extended Miles Davis's *Kind of Blue* into something more intense, more spiritual, and more harmonically open. Bill Frisell is the single outside voice here — a post-bop guitarist whose atmospheric, spacious approach to jazz makes him a logical reinterpretive voice for this Coltrane-quartet-rooted material, even though he was not a member of the group.

## Jimmy Garrison

Jimmy Garrison is a bassist associated with modal jazz and post-bop. He joined the Coltrane Quartet in 1961 and remained until Coltrane's death in 1967. His bass approach in the quartet context relied on long, sustained notes and pedal points that anchored the music during Coltrane and Tyner's most adventurous harmonic departures — a role that required as much restraint as technique. He died in 1976.

## Bill Frisell

Bill Frisell is a guitarist associated with jazz, Americana, avant-garde, and post-bop. He has collaborated with John Zorn, Paul Motian, Eberhard Weber, Charlie Haden, and many others, developing a distinctive sound built on subtle use of sustain, tremolo, and delay alongside a melodic language that draws on country, folk, and jazz without being reducible to any of them.

## Elvin Jones

Elvin Jones is a drummer associated with hard bop and modal jazz. He was the drummer for the Coltrane Classic Quartet from 1960 to 1966, during which he developed a polyrhythmic, multi-directional approach that fundamentally changed what jazz drumming could be. He played three against four, layered multiple rhythmic streams simultaneously, and engaged with the soloists' improvisation in real time rather than simply marking time. He died in 2004.

## McCoy Tyner

McCoy Tyner is a keyboard player associated with hard bop and modal jazz. He was the pianist for the Coltrane Classic Quartet from 1960 to 1965, developing a distinctive approach built on quartal voicings (chords built in fourths rather than thirds), pentatonic improvisation, and a powerful left-hand rhythmic drive that is immediately recognizable. He has led a prolific solo career since leaving the quartet. He died in 2020.

## John Coltrane

John Coltrane is a saxophonist associated with hard bop, modal jazz, and avant-garde jazz. His development between *Giant Steps* (1959) and *Interstellar Space* (1967) — from the most advanced bebop harmonic thinking through modal openness to free improvisation — is one of the most documented and analyzed trajectories in jazz history. His influence on saxophone playing, jazz composition, and the relationship between technique and spirituality is without parallel. He died in 1967.
