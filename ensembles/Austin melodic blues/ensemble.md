# Ensemble: Austin melodic blues

## Context

This ensemble draws from the Austin, Texas blues-rock and melodic rock scene centered on Stevie Ray Vaughan's Double Trouble band — with Eric Johnson and Blues Saraceno as related guitarists from the same geographic and stylistic orbit. The core Double Trouble rhythm section (Tommy Shannon on bass, Chris Layton on drums, Reese Wynans on keyboards) defined the Texas blues sound of the 1980s. Kyle Brock is a Texas blues-rock bassist from the same scene. Eric Johnson is an Austin guitarist whose clean-tone melodic approach overlaps with SRV's sphere. Blues Saraceno is a California-based guitarist whose early career was neoclassical shred but who later moved toward blues and rock.

## Kyle Brock

Kyle Brock is a Texas blues-rock and fusion bassist who has worked in the Austin and broader Texas music scene. He has been associated with Arc Angels (the band formed by Double Trouble alumni and guitarists Charlie Sexton and Doyle Bramhall II) and various Texas blues-rock contexts.

## Eric Johnson

Eric Johnson (b. 1954, Austin) is a Texas guitarist whose playing occupies a different zone from SRV: cleaner, more stratospheric, with an emphasis on melodic legato, violin-tone picking precision, and jazz-influenced harmony. His 1990 album *Ah Via Musicom* produced the Grammy-winning instrumental "Cliffs of Dover." He is meticulous about tone — his vintage Strats, specific cable brands, and battery types are part of a carefully managed signal chain.

## Chris Layton

Chris Layton (b. 1955, Corpus Christi TX) is the drummer of Double Trouble, Stevie Ray Vaughan's band, and one of the defining Texas blues-rock drummers. His playing is powerful, syncopated, and perfectly calibrated for the shuffle and straight-eighth feels that SRV's music requires. After Vaughan's death he has continued with Double Trouble (now with other guitarists) and session work.

## Reese Wynans

Reese Wynans (b. 1947) joined Stevie Ray Vaughan and Double Trouble in 1985 as a keyboardist, expanding the band from a trio to a quartet. He brought Hammond B3 organ, piano, and synth pads that added harmonic richness and depth to SRV's guitar-led sound. His role was always supportive — he never overshadowed the guitar — but his organ fills and chord coloring are an integral part of the *Soul to Soul* through *In Step* period sound.
