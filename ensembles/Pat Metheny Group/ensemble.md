# Ensemble: Pat Metheny Group

## Context

The Pat Metheny Group was founded in 1977 on ECM Records, built around guitarist Pat Metheny and keyboardist-composer Lyle Mays, whose long partnership defined the ensemble's warmly melodic, harmonically rich take on jazz fusion. Early albums featured Jaco Pastorius on bass and Bob Moses on drums before the PMG proper took shape with Mark Egan, Danny Gottlieb, and then later Steve Rodby and Paul Wertico through the Geffen and Nonesuch years. The group evolved through multiple lineups across more than three decades, incorporating Brazilian percussion, wordless vocals, and electronic orchestration while maintaining a distinctly ECM-influenced lyricism that set it apart from harder-edged fusion contemporaries.

## Jaco Pastorius

Jaco Pastorius (1951–1987) was an American bassist who revolutionized the role of the electric bass in jazz, most famously through his fretless technique, use of harmonics, and long melodic lines that functioned as a second voice rather than a time-keeping foundation. He first recorded with Pat Metheny on the 1976 ECM debut *Bright Size Life*, a trio session that showcased his singing fretless tone alongside Metheny and drummer Bob Moses. He then joined Weather Report in 1976, where his tenure on albums such as *Heavy Weather* (1977) and *8:30* (1979) brought him to international prominence. His self-titled 1976 solo album remains a landmark of the instrument, and his Word of Mouth big band project of the early 1980s extended his compositional ambitions into large-ensemble writing.

## Pat Metheny

Pat Metheny (born 1954) is an American guitarist from Lee's Summit, Missouri, who emerged as a major voice in jazz at age twenty-one when his ECM debut *Bright Size Life* appeared in 1976. He co-founded the Pat Metheny Group with keyboardist Lyle Mays in 1977, and the two composed and arranged virtually all of the group's music across more than thirty years. Beyond the group, Metheny has recorded in many other contexts — a free-jazz collaboration with Ornette Coleman (*Song X*, 1986), duo and trio recordings, and his solo *Orchestrion* project (2010), which used mechanically actuated instruments controlled from his guitar. He has won more Grammy Awards than any other artist in both jazz and pop categories combined, and his tone — characterized by a warm archtop sound, sophisticated harmonic vocabulary, and use of guitar synthesizer — is among the most recognizable in modern jazz.

## Peter Erskine

Peter Erskine (born 1954) is an American drummer from South Orange, New Jersey, who trained in the big bands of Stan Kenton and Maynard Ferguson before joining Weather Report in 1978. His four years with Weather Report — spanning the albums *Mr. Gone* (1978), *8:30* (1979), *Night Passage* (1980), and *Weather Report* (1982) — established him as one of jazz fusion's most nuanced drummers, able to balance propulsive groove with sophisticated brushwork and dynamic control. After leaving Weather Report, he worked with the Jaco Pastorius Word of Mouth big band and later became a founding member of Steps Ahead, the post-bop quintet featuring Michael Brecker and Mike Mainieri. Erskine has appeared on more than seven hundred recordings across jazz, pop, and orchestral contexts, and has been a professor of studio/jazz drumming at USC Thornton School of Music.

## Joe Zawinul

Joe Zawinul (1932–2007) was an Austrian-American keyboardist and composer who became one of the foundational figures of jazz fusion. After emigrating to the United States in 1959, he spent nine years as pianist in the Cannonball Adderley Quintet, contributing the gospel-flavored composition "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy" (1966) that became a crossover hit. In 1969 he contributed the title composition to Miles Davis's landmark *In a Silent Way* album, then co-founded Weather Report with Wayne Shorter in 1971, leading it until 1986 across more than fifteen albums. Zawinul was an early adopter of synthesizers as primary instruments rather than novelties, and his layered keyboard textures — spanning Fender Rhodes, Oberheim, and custom Yamaha synths — defined the harmonic language of Weather Report throughout its run. He founded the Zawinul Syndicate after Weather Report disbanded and continued recording and touring internationally until shortly before his death.

## Wayne Shorter

Wayne Shorter (1933–2023) was an American saxophonist and composer whose writing became central to the jazz canon across multiple decades and ensembles. After early work with Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers (1959–1963), he served as music director and principal composer for Miles Davis's Second Great Quintet (1964–1970), contributing compositions including "Footprints," "Nefertiti," and "E.S.P." that redefined what a jazz composition could be. He co-founded Weather Report with Joe Zawinul in 1971 and continued as its co-leader until 1986, then formed the Wayne Shorter Quartet with Danilo Perez, John Patitucci, and Brian Blade — widely regarded as one of the most important small groups in contemporary jazz. His Blue Note recordings as a leader in the 1960s, particularly *Speak No Evil* (1964) and *JuJu* (1964), remain landmark albums of the hard bop and post-bop periods.
