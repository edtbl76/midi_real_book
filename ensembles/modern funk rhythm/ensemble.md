# Ensemble: modern funk rhythm

## Context

Level 42 — the British jazz-funk band led by Mark King's extraordinary slap bass technique — defined a sophisticated strain of pop-funk in the 1980s, with Phil Gould's metronomic drumming and Mike Lindup's keyboards providing the harmonic and rhythmic framework. Cory Wong represents the contemporary end of this tradition, bringing Vulfpeck's stripped-back, extremely tight funk approach to guitar with an emphasis on right-hand percussive precision and clean, staccato articulation.

## Mark King

Mark King is the vocalist and bassist of Level 42, widely regarded as one of the fastest and most precise slap bass players in the world. His technique combines thumb-slap on the lower strings with rapid pull-offs on the upper strings to create a machine-gun density of notes that nevertheless remains musical and in service of the song. King's approach had a massive influence on pop-funk bass playing in the 1980s and beyond.

## Cory Wong

Cory Wong is a Minneapolis-based guitarist best known as a member of Vulfpeck, where his right-hand precision and staccato funk guitar playing helped define the band's stripped-back, extremely tight sound. His solo work and collaborations extend to pop, jazz, and big-band settings. Wong's playing is characterized by almost metronomic rhythmic accuracy and a clean, bright tone that emphasizes the percussive quality of the attack.

## Phil Gould

Phil Gould was Level 42's original drummer from 1980 through 1987, a founding member whose precise, funk-inflected groove playing was integral to the band's sound. His drumming combined jazz-influenced hi-hat work with a locked-in kick-and-snare pattern that perfectly complemented Mark King's slap bass. After health issues led him to step away in 1987, he returned to the band in later years.

## Mike Lindup

Mike Lindup is Level 42's keyboardist and co-vocalist, whose bright, clean synthesizer and piano work provided the harmonic color for the band's pop-funk arrangements. His falsetto vocals and keyboard parts are central to Level 42's sonic identity, bridging the jazz-funk roots with pop accessibility. Lindup's parts range from hard-edged synth bass-support to open, pianistic harmonic layers.
