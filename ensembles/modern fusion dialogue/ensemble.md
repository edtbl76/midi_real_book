# Ensemble: modern fusion dialogue

## Context

This ensemble centers on modern electric jazz fusion from the 1990s and 2000s, built around the intersection of jazz composition, extended technique, and groove-based improvisation. Matt Garrison and Gene Lake represent a generation of rhythm section players schooled in acoustic jazz who brought that vocabulary into electric contexts. Herbie Hancock provides the living bridge from hard bop to electric fusion, while Greg Howe and Ravi Coltrane bring contrasting voices — rock-inflected guitar fusion and post-bop saxophone — into dialogue with the rhythm section.

## Matt Garrison

Matt Garrison is a bassist and the son of John Coltrane's bassist Jimmy Garrison. He studied at Berklee and later with Jaco Pastorius student Wayne Shorter bassist John Patitucci, developing a highly refined approach to electric bass that integrates jazz compositional thinking with extended technique. Garrison has worked extensively as a sideman in modern jazz and fusion contexts, and his solo records show a deeply personal approach to groove, harmony, and texture.

## Greg Howe

Greg Howe is a self-taught guitarist from Indiana who released his debut Shrapnel Records album in 1988 and subsequently developed into one of the most technically gifted players in fusion-rock guitar. His playing is characterized by high-velocity legato runs, rhythmic sequencing, unusual harmonic choices, and the ability to play lines that sound more like horn phrases than traditional guitar vocabulary. He has worked extensively as a session guitarist for major pop artists while maintaining a prolific solo career.

## Gene Lake

Gene Lake is a New York-based drummer who has worked extensively as a sideman in modern jazz, R&B, and electric fusion contexts. He was a core member of Steve Coleman's M-Base collective and worked with guitarist Mike Stern and bassist Victor Bailey among others. Lake's playing combines M-Base rhythmic complexity with groove authority, making him adaptable across multiple contemporary jazz and fusion settings.

## Herbie Hancock

Herbie Hancock is one of jazz's most important pianists and composers, having recorded with Miles Davis's second great quintet in the mid-1960s before embarking on a massively influential solo career that encompassed hard bop, early electronic jazz, funk-fusion with the Headhunters, and pop-oriented crossover work. His harmonic imagination, compositional mastery, and openness to synthesizers and new technology make him a foundational figure across multiple styles.

## Ravi Coltrane

Ravi Coltrane is a tenor and soprano saxophonist and the son of John Coltrane and pianist Alice Coltrane. He emerged as an independent voice in the 1990s post-bop scene, developing his own vocabulary that acknowledges but is distinct from his father's legacy. Ravi Coltrane has recorded for Savoy Jazz and RCA Victor, working with musicians including Elvin Jones (his father's drummer), Jack DeJohnette, and others in the post-bop and contemporary jazz worlds.
