# Ensemble: King Curtis / Aretha

## Context

This ensemble represents the Atlantic Records soul and R&B session orbit of the late 1960s and early 1970s, anchored by King Curtis as Aretha Franklin's musical director. The players appeared together on landmark recordings including *Aretha Live at the Fillmore West* (1971), where Curtis led the band through one of the greatest live soul performances on record. Several members — Cornell Dupree, Eric Gale, Richard Tee, and Chuck Rainey — later formed Stuff, the definitive New York session groove band of the 1970s.

## Chuck Rainey

Chuck Rainey is one of the most recorded session bassists in history, known for his economical pocket, precise articulation, and ability to serve a song without calling attention to himself. He anchored Aretha Franklin's Atlantic sessions from the late 1960s onward and later became the bass voice on some of Steely Dan's most celebrated records.

## Cornell Dupree

Cornell Dupree was the definitive Atlantic Records rhythm guitarist of the late 1960s and 1970s — clean, unhurried, and perpetually in the pocket. His understated style made him nearly invisible in the mix, which was precisely the point: he made every session feel better without drawing attention to himself.

## Bernard Purdie

Bernard "Pretty" Purdie is one of the most recorded drummers in history, with an encyclopedic feel for R&B, soul, and funk. He is the originator of the "Purdie Shuffle" — a half-time feel with a ghosted triplet pattern on the snare — which became foundational to the sound of Steely Dan's *Aja* and widely influential across R&B and hip-hop production.

## Richard Tee

Richard Tee was a New York session pianist and organist whose gospel-rooted warmth and unfailing sense of groove made him one of the most sought-after keyboard players in the Atlantic Records universe and beyond. His piano work with Aretha Franklin and Paul Simon, and his organ with Stuff, defined the keyboard sound of 1970s New York soul.

## King Curtis

King Curtis (né Curtis Ousley, 1934–1971) was a Texas-born tenor saxophonist who became one of the most recorded musicians in R&B history. As Aretha Franklin's musical director, he shaped the sound of her Atlantic Records peak years and led the band on her landmark *Live at the Fillmore West* recording. He was murdered in New York City on August 13, 1971, ending a career that had touched nearly every corner of American popular music.
