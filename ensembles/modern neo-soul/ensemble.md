# Ensemble: modern neo-soul

## Context

D'Angelo's *Voodoo* (2000) and *Black Messiah* (2014) define the center of this ensemble's musical world — records that merged classic soul, funk, and jazz into something deeply personal and rhythmically radical. Pino Palladino was the bassist on both albums; Questlove was the drummer on both. John Mayer and Steve Jordan form the John Mayer Trio, which operates in a blues-soul space adjacent to this world. Roy Hargrove bridged jazz improvisation and neo-soul through his RH Factor project. Mateus Asato represents the social-media generation of melodic guitarists whose neo-soul voice has emerged primarily through online presence.

## Pino Palladino

Pino Palladino is a Welsh bassist who began his career as a session musician known for his fretless bass tone on early 1980s pop records (Paul Young, Don Henley). In the 1990s and 2000s he reinvented himself as one of the premier session bassists for soul, R&B, and rock, recording with D'Angelo (*Voodoo*, *Black Messiah*), John Mayer, The Who, and many others. His sense of groove and his ability to find the perfect note with the perfect timing in any style makes him one of the most in-demand bassists of his generation.

## John Mayer

John Mayer emerged in the early 2000s as a pop-blues guitarist whose technique far exceeded his radio profile suggested. His vocabulary is built on Stevie Ray Vaughan's Texas blues, filtered through his own harmonic sophistication from Berklee training, with an ability to sound both pop-accessible and authentically bluesy. The John Mayer Trio records (*Try!*, *Where the Light Is*) reveal the full depth of his blues-rock playing outside pop context.

## Mateus Asato

Mateus Asato is a Brazilian guitarist based in Los Angeles who first came to wide attention through Instagram videos demonstrating his melodic neo-soul guitar approach. His playing is characterized by vocal-quality phrasing, lyrical vibrato, and an emotional directness that communicates instantly in short-form video as well as in full recordings. He has toured with Tori Kelly and done session work across pop and neo-soul contexts.

## Questlove

Questlove (Ahmir Thompson) is the drummer and co-founder of The Roots, the Philadelphia hip-hop band that functions as house band for The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon. Beyond hip-hop and R&B, Questlove has worked extensively as a producer and session drummer in neo-soul contexts, most importantly as the drummer on D'Angelo's *Voodoo* (2000) and *Black Messiah* (2014). His drumming is deeply groove-focused, with a behind-the-beat feel and a sensitivity to silence and space that is central to the D'Angelo records' feel.

## Steve Jordan

Steve Jordan is a New York-based drummer whose career spans decades of session work and touring. He worked extensively with Saturday Night Live's house band in the early 1980s, formed the John Mayer Trio with Mayer and Palladino, and has been a long-time collaborator with Keith Richards in the X-Pensive Winos. Jordan's feel is rooted in R&B and rock — loose enough to swing but precise enough to anchor any arrangement.

## D'Angelo

D'Angelo (Michael Archer) is a singer, songwriter, multi-instrumentalist, and producer from Richmond, Virginia, whose two major studio albums — *Brown Sugar* (1995), *Voodoo* (2000), and *Black Messiah* (2014) — collectively helped define neo-soul as a genre and redefined what R&B could sound like rhythmically and harmonically. He plays piano, guitar, bass, and drums, and often performed all or most parts on his recordings. D'Angelo's approach to rhythm, particularly the extreme behind-the-beat feel on *Voodoo*, was radical and became highly influential.

## Roy Hargrove

Roy Hargrove was a Dallas-born trumpeter who worked across jazz, soul, and funk throughout his career until his death in 2018 at age 49. He appeared on D'Angelo's *Voodoo*, played on multiple Erykah Badu sessions, and led his own RH Factor, a funk-jazz group that brought his jazz trumpet into direct contact with soul and hip-hop rhythms. Hargrove had a precise, burnished trumpet tone and an ability to phrase with soul vocabulary — not just jazz language — that made him uniquely suited to neo-soul contexts.
