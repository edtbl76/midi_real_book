# Ensemble: Rainbow

## Context

Rainbow was founded by guitarist Ritchie Blackmore in 1975 after his departure from Deep Purple, initially recruiting the American band Elf—led by Ronnie James Dio—as his backing group. The band became known for a rotating cast of singers, bassists, and drummers across four distinct eras: the Dio years (1975–1979), the Graham Bonnet year (1979–1980), the Joe Lynn Turner years (1981–1984), and a 2015–2016 revival, while Blackmore remained the sole constant. Musically, Rainbow fused hard rock energy with classical and medieval European melodic motifs, a combination that made the band foundational to what would later be called neoclassical metal.

## Bob Daisley

Bob Daisley is an Australian bassist who was a member of Rainbow from 1975 through the recording of *Down to Earth* (1979), appearing on the debut album *Ritchie Blackmore's Rainbow*, *Rising*, *Long Live Rock 'n' Roll*, and *Down to Earth*. He contributed melodic, harmonically active bass lines that supported both the heavy riffing and the more orchestrated passages that defined the Dio and Bonnet eras. After leaving Rainbow, Daisley became one of rock's most in-demand session bassists, most notably co-writing and recording the first two Ozzy Osbourne solo albums, *Blizzard of Ozz* and *Diary of a Madman*, including the lyrics to "Crazy Train" and "Mr. Crowley." He has remained active across multiple decades of hard rock and heavy metal, contributing to records by Gary Moore, Ozzy Osbourne, and others. His style is rooted in melodic root-and-fifth bass playing with well-placed fills that frame the guitar without competing with it.

## Ritchie Blackmore

Ritchie Blackmore is an English guitarist who founded Rainbow in 1975 after departing Deep Purple, and remained the band's sole constant member across every lineup change and era through to the 2015 revival. His playing vocabulary blends blues-rooted pentatonic phrasing with European classical and Baroque melodic shapes — scales derived from Dorian and Phrygian modes, Bach-like sequenced runs, and dramatic minor-key harmonic movement — and he developed much of this language during his Deep Purple years before refining it further in Rainbow. He is one of the primary architects of what became neoclassical heavy metal, directly influencing players including Yngwie Malmsteen and Randy Rhoads. His rhythm guitar playing is equally important: he pioneered riff construction that layered classical interval structures over hard rock power chord energy. Since the mid-1990s he has led Blackmore's Night, a Renaissance and medieval folk project with his partner Candice Night.

## Tommy Aldridge

Tommy Aldridge is an American drummer known for powerful, theatrical hard rock playing — particularly his ability to control dynamic range across long sets and his signature use of bare-hand drumming in extended live solos. He came to prominence in the mid-1970s with the Pat Travers Band and Black Oak Arkansas before joining Ozzy Osbourne's band from 1981 to 1983, appearing on the live album *Speak of the Devil* and associated tours following Randy Rhoads's death. He subsequently spent extended periods with Whitesnake, playing on the self-titled 1987 album and related tours. Though not a member of Rainbow, his work with Ozzy Osbourne places him directly adjacent to the Rainbow lineage — the repertoire overlapped, the players overlapped (he worked alongside Don Airey in Whitesnake), and his hard rock drumming approach developed in the same sonic tradition.

## Don Airey

Don Airey is an English keyboard player who joined Rainbow in 1979 for the *Down to Earth* album (the Graham Bonnet era), contributing organ, synthesizer, and piano parts that bridged the classical keyboard grandeur of the Tony Carey era with a more streamlined, commercial sound. He has been one of the most active session and band keyboardists in British rock, also appearing on Ozzy Osbourne's *Blizzard of Ozz* (playing the organ on "Mr. Crowley"), Gary Moore recordings, Judas Priest's *Screaming for Vengeance*, and many others before eventually joining Deep Purple as a permanent member in 2002. His keyboard style draws on the Hammond organ tradition, classical piano, and synthesizer orchestration, and he can function both as a lead voice and as a harmonic support instrument within a heavy rock context. On the Rainbow recordings, his most important contribution is the keyboard architecture of *Down to Earth* — the album that produced "Since You Been Gone" and "All Night Long" — where his parts hold the harmonic center while Blackmore's guitar takes the lead.
