# Ensemble: Shrapnel shred

## Context

Shrapnel Records, founded by Mike Varney in 1980, defined the 1980s shred guitar genre by signing virtuoso players including Steve Vai, Paul Gilbert, Jason Becker, Marty Friedman, Tony MacAlpine, and Vinnie Moore. The label specialized in instrumental and hard rock guitar-forward records that demanded extreme technical facility, neoclassical phrasing, and high-speed alternate picking. Its alumni populated the wider hard rock world through David Lee Roth, Mr. Big, and Megadeth.

## Billy Sheehan

Billy Sheehan (b. 1953) is a hard-rock bassist who developed a three-finger picking technique, extensive tapping, and lead-bass phrasing that gave the bass voice a role normally reserved for guitar. He rose through Talas in the 1970s-early 1980s, joined David Lee Roth's band for the "Eat 'Em and Smile" tour (1986-1988), co-founded Mr. Big (1988), and has led Niacin alongside drummer Dennis Chambers.

## Jason Becker

Jason Becker (b. 1969, Richmond, CA) was signed to Shrapnel at 16 and formed Cacophony with Marty Friedman, producing two albums of neoclassical shred before releasing his solo debut "Perpetual Burn" (1988) — widely considered one of the most technically advanced guitar albums of the decade. He was hired to replace Steve Vai in David Lee Roth's band in 1990 but was diagnosed with ALS that same year; he has not played guitar since the early 1990s and now composes music via eye-movement software.

## Mike Mangini

Mike Mangini (b. 1963, Boston, MA) held speed drumming world records before joining Steve Vai's touring band in the mid-1990s and Extreme. He replaced Mike Portnoy in Dream Theater in 2011 following a public audition involving multiple world-class drummers. His playing emphasizes metric precision and polyrhythmic control, and he has been the primary drummer on every Dream Theater record since "A Dramatic Turn of Events" (2011).

## Michael Keneally

Michael Keneally (b. 1961, San Diego, CA) served as Frank Zappa's last guitarist and straw-boss (1988-1993), playing rhythm guitar, lead guitar, and keyboards simultaneously or in rotation across complex repertoire. After Zappa's death he launched a solo career with "Hat" (1992) and has recorded prolifically, collaborating with Steve Vai, Andy Partridge, and many others while developing a compositional voice that draws on rock, jazz, classical, and humor in roughly equal measure.
