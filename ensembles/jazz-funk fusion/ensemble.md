# Ensemble: jazz-funk fusion

## Context

Béla Fleck and the Flecktones are one of the most genuinely original ensembles in American music — a band that fuses jazz, bluegrass, funk, and world music through Fleck's boundary-dissolving banjo playing and Victor Wooten's extraordinary bass technique. Howard Levy's chromatic harmonica playing (which he largely invented as a modern jazz instrument) adds a further unusual timbral dimension. Oz Noy and Dennis Chambers bring a more conventional jazz-funk fusion vocabulary that connects this ensemble to the mainstream New York jazz scene, while Dennis Chambers's funk credentials — rooted in Parliament-Funkadelic — give the groove side of the ensemble maximum credibility.

## Victor Wooten

Victor Wooten is a bassist associated with slap bass, fusion, R&B, and bluegrass. He is one of the most technically complete electric bassists alive, with a double-thumb slap technique that allows continuous 16th-note patterns and an ability to play melody, harmony, and bass line simultaneously. He is a founding member of Béla Fleck and the Flecktones and has released several solo albums.

## Oz Noy

Oz Noy is a guitarist associated with jazz fusion and funk. He was born in Israel and developed his playing partly in the Tel Aviv jazz scene before relocating to New York, where he became a prolific sideman and session player. His approach combines deep jazz harmony with funky right-hand technique and a distorted tone that gives his lines an aggressive edge.

## Dennis Chambers

Dennis Chambers is a drummer associated with jazz fusion and funk. He came up in Parliament-Funkadelic, giving him a deep funk foundation that underlies all his later jazz-fusion work. He has recorded with John Scofield, Mike Stern, Santana, CAB, and Oz Noy, adapting his approach to each context while maintaining his signature combination of groove authority and technical precision.

## Bela Fleck

Béla Fleck is a banjo player associated with jazz, bluegrass, classical, and world music. He has systematically expanded the banjo's perceived genre boundaries — taking the instrument through bluegrass, progressive country, fusion, African music, classical concerto, and back — without any of it sounding forced. The Flecktones are the primary vehicle for his jazz-funk explorations, but his solo work ranges from Bach to collaborative African recordings.

## Howard Levy

Howard Levy is a harmonica player associated with jazz fusion, blues, and world music. He largely invented the chromatic approach to the diatonic harmonica — enabling jazz-level harmony and improvisation on an instrument previously thought to be too limited for that purpose. He was a founding member of the Flecktones, and his departure in 1992 left a distinctive gap in the band's sound.
