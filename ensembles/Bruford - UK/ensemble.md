# Ensemble: Bruford / UK

## Context

This file covers two overlapping late-1970s ensembles: **Bruford**, Bill Bruford's jazz-fusion band (1977–1980), which released three studio albums and featured Jeff Berlin and Allan Holdsworth; and **UK** (1978–1979), a short-lived progressive-rock supergroup co-founded by Bruford and John Wetton that also included Allan Holdsworth and Eddie Jobson. Both bands emerged from the post-King Crimson / post-Yes fusion scene and are united by Bruford's compositional voice and a shared commitment to technical complexity in a rock context.

## Jeff Berlin

Jeff Berlin is one of the most technically accomplished electric bassists of the fusion era, known for his clean articulation, advanced harmonic vocabulary, and melodic independence. He was the bassist throughout the Bruford band's run, appearing on all three studio albums: *Feels Good to Me* (1977), *One of a Kind* (1979), and *Gradually Going Tornado* (1980).

## Allan Holdsworth

Allan Holdsworth (1946–2017) was one of the most technically and harmonically innovative guitarists of any era. His legato technique — using hammer-ons and pull-offs to create a near-wind-instrument smoothness — and his unusual scale and chord choices influenced players as different as Eddie Van Halen and Frank Zappa. He played in the Bruford band (*Feels Good to Me*, *One of a Kind*) and on UK's debut album, before leaving to pursue his solo career.

## Bill Bruford

Bill Bruford (b. 1949) is one of progressive rock's most influential drummers. He left Yes in 1972 at the height of their commercial success to join King Crimson, a move that defined his career-long preference for musical risk over commercial reward. His own Bruford band (1977–1980) and his involvement in UK (1978) represent the peak of his jazz-fusion work. He retired from music in 2009.

## Jan Hammer

Jan Hammer (b. 1948) is a Czech-American keyboardist and composer best known as a founding member of the Mahavishnu Orchestra (1971–1976) and for his *Miami Vice* television theme (1984). He is included here as an orbit player whose Mahavishnu Orchestra work shares essential DNA with the Bruford / UK world — the same late-1970s fusion vocabulary, the same ambition to push rock rhythm into jazz-complexity territory. Hammer was not a member of the Bruford band or UK; UK's keyboardist was Eddie Jobson.

## Eddie Jobson

Eddie Jobson (b. 1955) is a keyboardist and electric violinist who served as UK's primary keyboard and violin voice across both of their albums. Before UK he had been a member of Curved Air and Roxy Music; his combination of classical violin training and synthesizer fluency made him one of the distinctive voices in late-1970s progressive rock.
