# Ensemble: hard-bop blues

## Context

This ensemble gathers players from the 1950s and 1960s hard-bop and post-bop world, centered on Charles Mingus's compositional authority and his working bands of that era. Mingus's groups were unlike most jazz bands of the period — compositions were dense, rhythmically unpredictable, infused with gospel and blues, and demanded a level of musical engagement from every member that was unusual for its time. Eric Dolphy appeared on several of Mingus's most important records, and Jaki Byard was a regular keyboard voice in the later Mingus bands. Jim Hall and Kenny Burrell each brought their own distinct guitar voices to the blues-jazz tradition, and Max Roach was Mingus's primary collaborator in establishing hard bop as a genre.

## Charles Mingus

Charles Mingus is a bassist and composer associated with modern jazz, hard bop, blues, and gospel-inflected jazz. He is one of the most important composers in jazz history — his works range from intricate extended compositions to raw blues and gospel shouts, and his bass playing combined walking authority with unusual melodic invention. He died in 1979.

## Jim Hall

Jim Hall is a guitarist associated with jazz and cool jazz. His approach to the guitar was unusually restrained and contrapuntal for his era — where most jazz guitarists emphasized single-note improvisation, Hall integrated countermelodies, harmonic responses, and rhythmic displacement that made his guitar playing conversational in a chamber-music sense. He died in 2013.

## Kenny Burrell

Kenny Burrell is a guitarist associated with jazz, soul jazz, hard bop, and blues. His playing has an earthier, more blues-soaked quality than Jim Hall — the influence of T-Bone Walker and early blues guitar is audible in every phrase. He was a prolific Blue Note Records artist in the 1950s and 1960s and is still active as a performer and educator at UCLA.

## Max Roach

Max Roach is a drummer associated with bebop and hard bop. He is one of the architects of modern jazz drumming, most notable for moving melodic and harmonic responsibility to the cymbal — using the ride cymbal to carry the time while freeing the snare and bass drum for accents and conversation. He co-led the Max Roach–Clifford Brown Quintet, one of the foundational hard-bop groups. He died in 2007.

## Jaki Byard

Jaki Byard is a keyboard player associated with jazz. He played piano across an extraordinarily wide stylistic range — stride piano, bebop, free jazz, and gospel — sometimes within a single performance. He was a regular member of Mingus's working groups in the mid-1960s and appeared on several of Mingus's most important records of that period. He was murdered in 1999.

## Eric Dolphy

Eric Dolphy is a saxophonist (and flautist and bass clarinetist) associated with hard bop, post-bop, and avant-garde jazz. His playing on alto sax used wide intervals and sudden register leaps that sounded dissonant in context but resolved with harmonic logic — a personal extension of Charlie Parker that was more extreme than almost any contemporary. He appeared on Charles Mingus's most important 1960s records and recorded his masterpiece *Out to Lunch!* shortly before his death in 1964.
