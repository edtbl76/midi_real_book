# Ensemble: P-Funk / Buckethead

## Context

Parliament-Funkadelic (P-Funk) is the collective enterprise led by George Clinton, combining Parliament (the vocal/pop side) and Funkadelic (the rock/psychedelic side) with a shared pool of musicians. The core P-Funk ensemble defined funk from the early 1970s through the 1980s: Bootsy Collins's syncopated bass, Eddie Hazel's psychedelic guitar, Jerome Brailey's drumming, Bernie Worrell's Minimoog keyboard bass, and the horn section of Maceo Parker and Fred Wesley. Buckethead's connection to this world came through the 2004 "Bucket of Bernie Brains" project with Bernie Worrell, and his solo work shows clear P-Funk funk influence.

## Bootsy Collins

Bootsy Collins (b. 1951, Cincinnati) is one of the defining bass players in funk history. He came up playing with James Brown (1969–1971) before joining Parliament-Funkadelic, where his syncopated slap technique, space-aware placement, and iconic star-bass image became inseparable from the P-Funk sound. His bass lines are rhythmically precise, deeply syncopated, and leave enormous amounts of space — funk as much defined by silence as by notes.

## Eddie Hazel

Eddie Hazel (1950–1992) was the lead guitarist of Funkadelic and one of the most emotionally expressive guitarists in rock history. His playing blends psychedelic blues, Jimi Hendrix-influenced wah vocabulary, and soulful single-note phrasing. "Maggot Brain" (1971) — a 10-minute solo performed as if he had just heard his mother had died — is among the most celebrated guitar performances in any genre.

## Catfish Collins

Catfish Collins (1943–2010) is the rhythm guitarist of Funkadelic and older brother of Bootsy Collins. He was central to the James Brown band (as part of the JBs) before joining Parliament-Funkadelic. His right-hand rhythm technique — percussive, muted, deeply syncopated — is a model for funk rhythm guitar and closely connected to the Clyde Stubblefield/James Brown tradition.

## Buckethead

Buckethead (b. 1969) connects to the P-Funk world most directly through the 2004 "Bucket of Bernie Brains" project with Bernie Worrell. His solo catalog also contains funk-influenced tracks — particularly from the *Giant Robot* and *Pepper's Ghost* eras — that show absorption of P-Funk's groove language into his own high-speed experimental idiom.

## Jerome Brailey

Jerome Brailey (b. 1950) was the drummer for Parliament-Funkadelic from 1972 to 1977, appearing on key albums including *Mothership Connection* (1975) and *Chocolate City* (1975). His drumming is funk-precise, supporting the groove without calling attention to itself — a model of how to hold a complex ensemble together from the bottom.

## Bernie Worrell

Bernie Worrell (1944–2016) was the founding keyboardist of Parliament-Funkadelic and one of the most important keyboard players in the history of funk and rock. He introduced the Minimoog as a bass instrument, replacing the need for a conventional bass guitar in several iconic P-Funk tracks. He later played with Talking Heads and collaborated with Buckethead on the 2004 "Bucket of Bernie Brains" project.

## Maceo Parker

Maceo Parker (b. 1943, Kinston NC) is an alto and tenor saxophonist best known for his work with James Brown (repeatedly from the 1960s onward) and Parliament-Funkadelic. His playing is blues-rooted, groove-focused, and immediately identifiable: short motivic phrases, clear articulation, and an instinct for exactly when to play and when to stop. He has also led an acclaimed solo career.

## Fred Wesley

Fred Wesley (b. 1943, Mobile AL) is a trombonist and arranger who was the musical director of the James Brown band through the 1970s and a key member of Parliament-Funkadelic. He is the primary arranger of the P-Funk horn section and is responsible for much of its vocabulary: short stabs, call-and-response with vocals, and melodic counter-lines against the groove. He also leads his own jazz groups.
