# Ensemble: abstract fusion

## Context

Tribal Tech, the primary vehicle for Scott Henderson and Gary Willis, redefined jazz-fusion by grafting Henderson's blues-saturated improvisation onto a rhythmically adventurous funk framework — a sound that is neither pure jazz nor pure rock but something harder to categorize. Willis's fretless-influenced fingerstyle and Kirk Covington's elastic groove gave the band an unusual elasticity, while Scott Kinsey's synthesizer work pushed harmonic color beyond standard fusion vocabulary. Wayne Krantz and Dean Brown each bring independent bodies of work in abstract groove and funk-fusion that complement and contrast with the Tribal Tech approach.

## Gary Willis

Gary Willis is a bassist associated with jazz fusion. His fingerstyle technique emphasizes rhythmic displacement and precise articulation without a pick, creating a fluid, fretless-like quality on fretted bass. He co-led Tribal Tech from the mid-1980s through the 2000s and has released solo records that extend his interest in odd meters and harmonic density.

## Wayne Krantz

Wayne Krantz is a guitarist associated with jazz fusion and abstract groove music. His approach to improvisation is highly idiosyncratic: he frequently plays independent rhythmic patterns with his right hand against melodic lines, creating a layered, self-contained feel. He has led his own trio (with bassist Tim Lefebvre and drummer Keith Carlock) and has worked extensively as a sideman with Steely Dan's band.

## Kirk Covington

Kirk Covington is a drummer associated with jazz fusion and funk. He was the primary drummer for Tribal Tech across their core catalog, developing an elastic, interactive style that served Henderson's unpredictable phrasing without ever losing the groove foundation. His hi-hat work and kick drum placement are defining elements of the Tribal Tech sound.

## Scott Kinsey

Scott Kinsey is a keyboard player associated with jazz fusion. His synthesizer work is a defining textural element of Tribal Tech's later records — he uses layered analog and digital sounds to create dense harmonic clouds that sit behind Henderson's guitar without competing with it. He has also led his own records exploring extended fusion and electronic elements.
