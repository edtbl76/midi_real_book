# Ensemble: thrash metal lead

## Context

Metallica defined thrash metal from their San Francisco formation in 1981 through the classic era ending with *Master of Puppets* (1986) — with Cliff Burton's melodic, distorted bass approach and Lars Ulrich's aggressive double-kick drumming as the rhythm foundation, and James Hetfield's down-picked rhythm guitar and Kirk Hammett's wah-saturated leads as the guitar voices. Marty Friedman is included here not as a Metallica member — he was never in Metallica — but as the lead guitarist of Megadeth from 1990 to 1999, Metallica's primary thrash-era rival, contributing some of the era's most distinctive lead guitar work on *Rust in Peace* and *Countdown to Extinction*.

## Cliff Burton

Cliff Burton was Metallica's bassist from 1982 until his death in a tour bus accident in September 1986. He recorded three studio albums: *Kill 'Em All* (1983), *Ride the Lightning* (1984), and *Master of Puppets* (1986). Burton's approach to bass was radical in a thrash context — he treated the instrument as a lead voice, using a wah pedal for solos, playing melodic passages with distortion, and composing complex bass lines that functioned independently of the guitar riffs. His bass solo "Anesthesia (Pulling Teeth)" and the instrumental "Orion" remain touchstones of how bass guitar can function in heavy metal.

## James Hetfield

James Hetfield is Metallica's rhythm guitarist, co-founder, and vocalist. His rhythm guitar technique — particularly his down-picking, which involves picking every note with a downstroke rather than alternating up and down — generates enormous power and consistency at high speeds. Hetfield is responsible for some of thrash metal's most recognizable riffs, and his compositional partnership with Lars Ulrich produced the entire Metallica catalog through the band's classic period and beyond.

## Kirk Hammett

Kirk Hammett joined Metallica in 1983 as the band's lead guitarist, replacing Dave Mustaine. His lead guitar style is defined by heavy wah-pedal use, pentatonic and blues-scale vocabulary adapted to metal context, and an ability to write memorable guitar solos that feel like melodic events rather than technical displays. Hammett studied with Joe Satriani before joining Metallica, and his technique reflects that formal training while maintaining a metal-oriented energy.

## Marty Friedman

Marty Friedman is a guitarist from Washington DC who joined Megadeth in 1990 and appeared on four studio albums — *Rust in Peace* (1990), *Countdown to Extinction* (1992), *Youthanasia* (1994), and *Cryptic Writings* (1997) — before leaving the band in 1999 to pursue a career in Japan. His playing is characterized by a highly personal melodic approach using exotic scales (particularly Phrygian dominant and harmonic minor), wide-interval string skipping, and a distinctive vibrato that is slow, wide, and expressive rather than fast and tight. Friedman never recorded with Metallica.

## Lars Ulrich

Lars Ulrich is the Danish co-founder and drummer of Metallica, and the primary architect of the band's approach to heavy metal. His drumming is defined by double-bass-drum technique, a very dry, tight drum sound (particularly on early records), an ability to lock precisely with Hetfield's rhythm guitar, and a compositional sense that made the drums an equal partner in Metallica's arrangements rather than just a timekeeping backdrop. Ulrich's work on the first four Metallica albums established the thrash metal drum template.
