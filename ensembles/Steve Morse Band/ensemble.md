# Ensemble: Steve Morse Band

## Context

The Dixie Dregs formed in Georgia in the mid-1970s and pioneered a fusion of rock, bluegrass, country, and jazz that had no real precedent. Steve Morse founded the band and remained its creative center through albums including *Free Fall* (1977), *What If* (1978), *Night of the Living Dregs* (1979), and *Unsung Heroes* (1981), before forming the Steve Morse Band in 1983 when the Dregs went on hiatus. Rod Morgenstein and T Lavitz carried across from the Dregs into the Morse Band, and Dave LaRue became Morse's primary bassist for decades of ongoing work.

## Dave LaRue

Dave LaRue (born 1961) became Steve Morse's primary bassist through the Steve Morse Band era and continued with the Dixie Dregs through their reunion periods. He also served as original bassist in Planet X alongside Derek Sherinian, before Tony Levin and later Felix Fierabracci joined that project.

## Steve Morse

Steve Morse (born 1954) founded the Dixie Dregs while at the University of Miami and developed a guitar style that combines alternate-picked precision, wide-interval lines, country hybrid picking, classical passages, and jazz harmony in a way that remains largely unique. He replaced Ritchie Blackmore in Deep Purple in 1994 and remained with that band until 2022.

## Rod Morgenstein

Rod Morgenstein (born 1953) was the Dixie Dregs' drummer from the band's earliest recordings and remained through all of their reunion periods. He is also known for his work with Winger (1987–1994), which brought him to a mainstream rock audience. He later taught at Berklee College of Music.

## T Lavitz

T Lavitz (1956–2010) was the keyboard player throughout the Dixie Dregs' original run and continued with the Steve Morse Band. He brought a wide vocabulary to the band — organ, synthesizer, electric piano — that helped define the Dregs' sound as something more layered than guitar-driven fusion alone. He died April 7, 2010.

## Jerry Goodman

Jerry Goodman (born 1949) was the electric violinist in the original Mahavishnu Orchestra (1971–1973), one of the most influential fusion bands of the decade. His work on *The Inner Mounting Flame* (1971) and *Birds of Fire* (1973) helped establish the electric violin as a credible lead instrument in rock-influenced jazz. He later appeared with the Dixie Dregs.
