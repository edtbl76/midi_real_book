# Ensemble: Dave Weckl Band / GRP

## Context

The Dave Weckl Band and the GRP Records orbit represent the high-precision end of 1990s jazz fusion: technically demanding arrangements, studio-quality live playing, and a shared pool of elite sidemen that circulated between projects. Dave Grusin co-founded GRP Records in 1982 with Larry Rosen, making GRP the primary label home for Lee Ritenour's solo work and a defining institutional force in contemporary jazz; Weckl's solo band drew directly from that same world of players and production values. Tom Kennedy anchored the Weckl Band's bass chair from the mid-1990s onward, creating a rhythm section partnership built on interlocking precision and deep mutual familiarity.

## Tom Kennedy

Tom Kennedy is a bassist known for jazz-fusion technical fluency, clean high-register execution, and improvisational command. He served as the primary bassist in the Dave Weckl Band from the mid-1990s onward and has worked extensively with Mike Stern, John Scofield, and a wide range of contemporary jazz artists.

## Lee Ritenour

Lee Ritenour — nicknamed "Captain Fingers" — is one of the most recorded studio guitarists in history, credited on more than 3,000 sessions. A GRP Records mainstay from the label's early years, Ritenour's playing merges jazz-chord sophistication, lyrical lead phrasing, and extraordinary tonal control across acoustic and electric instruments.

## Dave Weckl

Dave Weckl is one of the most influential jazz-fusion drummers of the modern era. His work with the Chick Corea Elektric Band through the late 1980s and early 1990s set a new standard for precision, groove, and electronic integration in fusion drumming. His solo career, launched in 1990 on GRP, produced a string of highly detailed fusion recordings under the Dave Weckl Band name.

## Dave Grusin

Dave Grusin is a pianist, composer, and the co-founder (with Larry Rosen) of GRP Records, the label that defined polished contemporary jazz and jazz fusion from the early 1980s through the 1990s. A multiple Grammy winner and Academy Award nominee (On Golden Pond score), Grusin's playing integrates classical training, bebop vocabulary, and a composer's ear for orchestral color.
