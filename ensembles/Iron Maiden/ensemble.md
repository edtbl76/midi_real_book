# Ensemble: Iron Maiden

## Context

Iron Maiden was formed in Leyton, East London in 1975 by bassist and primary songwriter Steve Harris, and became one of the defining acts of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal. The classic lineup — Harris, guitarists Dave Murray and Adrian Smith, vocalist Bruce Dickinson, and drummer Nicko McBrain — established the sound that dominated their imperial period from 1982 through 1988 and again from 1999 onward with the addition of a third guitarist, Janick Gers. What makes the ensemble distinctive is Harris's galloping, pick-driven bass playing, which functions as both rhythmic engine and melodic voice; the twin (later triple) guitar harmony leads drawn from Thin Lizzy and classical music; and Dickinson's operatic, wide-ranging tenor that anchors the band's epic, narrative-driven songwriting.

## Steve Harris

Steve Harris was born on 12 March 1956 in Leytonstone, East London. He founded Iron Maiden in 1975 and has remained the band's bassist, primary songwriter, and de facto musical director throughout their entire career. Harris plays almost exclusively with a pick and is known for his distinctive "galloping" triplet bass lines — a technique that became the rhythmic signature of Iron Maiden's sound — as well as for his high-register melodic fills and countermelodies. He has written or co-written the majority of the band's catalogue, including extended prog-influenced epics such as "Rime of the Ancient Mariner" and "The Seventh Son of a Seventh Son." Outside of Iron Maiden, Harris has led the project British Lion, a straightforward hard rock band formed around singer Richard Taylor, which has released albums and toured independently of Maiden.

## Adrian Smith

Adrian Smith was born on 27 February 1957 in Hackney, East London. He joined Iron Maiden in 1980 in time to record Killers (1981) and went on to co-write some of the band's most successful songs, including "2 Minutes to Midnight," "Wasted Years," and "Can I Play with Madness." Smith is regarded as the more technically precise and compositionally structured of Maiden's two classic-era lead guitarists, with a style that leans toward articulate melodic phrasing rather than pure blues feel. He left the band in 1990 — reportedly over creative differences regarding the direction of No Prayer for the Dying — and during his absence formed the project A.S.A.P. and later collaborated with Dickinson on the solo albums Accident of Birth (1997) and The Chemical Wedding (1998), widely considered among the finest heavy metal records of that decade. He rejoined Iron Maiden in 1999 as part of the three-guitar lineup alongside Murray and Janick Gers.

## Nicko McBrain

Nicko McBrain was born Michael Henry McBrain on 5 June 1952 in Hackney, East London. He joined Iron Maiden in 1982, replacing Clive Burr, and made his debut on Piece of Mind (1983). McBrain's drumming is characterized by highly melodic tom work, an unconventional but deeply musical sense of fill placement, and a distinctive flowing quality that avoids the purely mechanical 4/4 pulse common to hard rock. He studied jazz drumming early in his career — he had previously played with French rock band Trust and Pat Travers — and that influence is audible in his cymbal placement and his tendency to phrase across the barline. His interplay with Harris's bass forms one of the most distinctive rhythm section partnerships in heavy metal, with the two often functioning as a single melodic-rhythmic unit.
