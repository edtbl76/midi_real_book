# Ensemble: Lee Ritenour band

## Context

Lee Ritenour is a Los Angeles-based guitarist who became one of the most prolific session and fusion players of the 1970s and 1980s, nicknamed "Captain Fingers" for his technical precision. His working band drew from the deep pool of LA session players and jazz-fusion musicians: Abraham Laboriel (bass), Vinnie Colaiuta (drums), Joe Sample (keyboards from the Crusaders), and Justo Almario (saxophone). The ensemble sound is polished West Coast jazz-fusion: clean tone, sophisticated harmony, and session-grade ensemble execution.

## Abraham Laboriel

Abraham Laboriel (b. 1947, Mexico City) is one of the most recorded bass players in Los Angeles session history. He combines Latin music roots, jazz fusion sophistication, and session adaptability across every genre. His tone is warm and round, his harmonic flexibility is extraordinary, and he has played on thousands of recordings from pop to orchestral. He is the father of drummer Abraham Laboriel Jr. (Paul McCartney's touring drummer).

## Lee Ritenour

Lee Ritenour (b. 1952, Los Angeles) is a jazz-fusion guitarist nicknamed "Captain Fingers" who became one of the most-recorded session guitarists in Los Angeles during the 1970s. His studio work spans pop, R&B, and jazz. His solo career is built on polished West Coast fusion: clean tone, chord-melody sophistication, and an appreciation for jazz guitar tradition (particularly Wes Montgomery). He co-founded GRP Records and has won multiple Grammy Awards.

## Vinnie Colaiuta

Vinnie Colaiuta (b. 1956) is widely considered one of the greatest session and ensemble drummers alive. He played with Frank Zappa (1978–1981) before becoming the most-recorded drummer in LA through the 1980s–1990s. His work with Lee Ritenour is a model of sophisticated fusion drumming: complex polyrhythms executed with groove, not just technique.

## Joe Sample

Joe Sample (1939–2014, Houston) was the founding pianist and musical director of the Jazz Crusaders (later the Crusaders). He became one of the most recorded keyboard players in Los Angeles history, contributing to soul, R&B, pop, and fusion recordings across five decades. His piano and Fender Rhodes work is characterized by warm, soulful voicings with a gospel underpinning that adapts seamlessly to jazz or pop contexts.

## Justo Almario

Justo Almario (b. 1950, Colombia) is a saxophonist and flutist who has been a fixture of the Los Angeles Christian jazz and fusion scene since the 1970s. He is a member of Koinonia (alongside Abraham Laboriel and Alex Acuña) and has worked with Lee Ritenour, Andraé Crouch, Al Jarreau, and many others. His saxophone work is smooth, lyrical, and session-professional — always serving the track.
