# Ensemble: Fourplay

## Context

Fourplay is a jazz-funk and smooth jazz supergroup formed in 1991, originally comprising Bob James (piano), Lee Ritenour (guitar), Harvey Mason (drums), and Nathan East (bass). Ritenour departed in 1997 and was replaced by Larry Carlton, whose blues-inflected Gibson ES-335 tone became central to the group's later identity. The band's albums — from the self-titled debut through "Silver" (2015) — document a deliberate craft: ensemble interplay over individual showboating, melodic hooks rooted in jazz harmony, and production that rewards close listening.

## Nathan East

Nathan East is one of the most recorded bassists in contemporary music, with credits spanning smooth jazz, pop, R&B, and rock. A Fourplay founding member, he has also served as Eric Clapton's touring bassist since 1984 and appeared on landmark sessions for Daft Punk, Beyoncé, and Michael Jackson. His playing is defined by impeccable time, melodic note choice, and a warm, rounded tone that anchors any arrangement without drawing attention away from it.

## Larry Carlton

Larry Carlton, known as "Mr. 335" for his Gibson ES-335, is one of the definitive session and studio guitarists of the 1970s and 1980s. His work with Steely Dan on albums like "The Royal Scam" and "Aja" established his reputation for jazz-blues phrasing delivered with studio precision. He joined Fourplay in 1998 after Lee Ritenour's departure, bringing a slightly bluesier, more harmonically adventurous guitar voice to the group.

## Harvey Mason

Harvey Mason is a Los Angeles session drummer and Fourplay founding member whose credits extend from Herbie Hancock's landmark "Head Hunters" album (1973) through decades of CTI and GRP records. His playing is defined by a deep, relaxed pocket, refined brush and stick technique, and an instinct for serving the arrangement rather than showcasing chops. As a Fourplay founding member, his groove is the rhythmic foundation all four players build from.

## Bob James

Bob James is a pianist, keyboardist, and arranger who became one of the defining figures of jazz-funk and smooth jazz through his CTI Records output in the 1970s and GRP Records work in the 1980s. His recording "Nautilus" (from "One," 1974) became one of the most sampled recordings in hip-hop history. As a Fourplay founder, he functions as the harmonic architect of the group — comping, voicing, and occasionally soloing in a style that bridges bebop sophistication with pop accessibility.

## Eric Marienthal

Eric Marienthal is a smooth jazz and fusion saxophonist best known as a long-standing member of Chick Corea's Elektric Band (1986–1994), where he developed a polished, technically fluent style built on bebop vocabulary delivered with smooth jazz accessibility. He has appeared as a guest in the Fourplay orbit and has maintained a prolific solo career on GRP and other labels since the late 1980s.
