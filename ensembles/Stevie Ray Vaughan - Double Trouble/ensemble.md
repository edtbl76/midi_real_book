# Ensemble: Stevie Ray Vaughan / Double Trouble

## Context

Stevie Ray Vaughan and Double Trouble were a Texas blues-rock trio whose recordings in the 1980s helped ignite a mainstream blues revival. Vaughan's guitar technique was built on heavy strings (typically .013s), open-E tuning, and a thumb-over-the-neck grip that let him play both rhythm and bass lines simultaneously. The core trio of Vaughan, bassist Tommy Shannon, and drummer Chris Layton defined the sound from the 1983 debut Texas Flood through the final studio album In Step (1989); keyboardist Reese Wynans joined in 1985 for Soul to Soul and stayed until Vaughan's death in 1990. This ensemble also includes players from the broader Texas blues orbit — Kyle Brock (Electromagnets, Eric Johnson Group), Eric Johnson, and Blues Saraceno — who share the same Austin-rooted vocabulary.

## Kyle Brock

Kyle Brock is an Austin-based bassist best known for his work with the jazz-rock fusion band Electromagnets in the mid-1970s, where he played alongside guitarist Eric Johnson. After the Electromagnets disbanded, he became the touring bassist for the Eric Johnson Group — a trio with drummer Tommy Taylor — and played bass on portions of Johnson's platinum 1990 album Ah Via Musicom. His feel is rooted in the Texas funk-fusion pocket that informed the Austin scene from which SRV also emerged. Brock is recognized for his singing, clean note definition, and ability to support a guitar-driven trio without crowding the lead voice.

## Stevie Ray Vaughan

Stevie Ray Vaughan (1954–1990) was a Texas blues guitarist who, with Double Trouble, revived mainstream interest in electric blues during the 1980s. He played heavy strings — typically .013s — on a battered 1963 Stratocaster he called Number One, and his tuning was usually a half-step down with a thumb-over-the-neck grip that gave him simultaneous access to bass-string rhythm and lead phrasing. His four studio albums with Double Trouble — Texas Flood (1983), Couldn't Stand the Weather (1984), Soul to Soul (1985), and In Step (1989) — define the Texas blues-rock canon. He also recorded Family Style (1990) with his brother Jimmie before dying in a helicopter crash after a concert in East Troy, Wisconsin.

## Eric Johnson

Eric Johnson is an Austin, Texas guitarist known for an obsessive approach to tone and a right-hand picking technique of extraordinary precision. He came up in the same Austin scene as SRV — both played at Antone's and shared bills regularly — and he led the jazz-rock fusion band Electromagnets in the mid-1970s before embarking on a solo career. His 1990 album Ah Via Musicom went platinum and won the Grammy for Best Rock Instrumental Performance for "Cliffs of Dover." Johnson pays direct tribute to his friend with "S.R.V.," a track on his 1996 album Venus Isle that features Jimmie Vaughan as a guest. He is included here as a peer voice in the Texas blues-rock orbit, not as a Double Trouble member.

## Blues Saraceno

Blues Saraceno (born 1971, Hartford, Connecticut) is an American guitarist who was discovered at 16 and released his debut instrumental album Never Look Back in 1989 on Guitar Recordings. Two more albums followed — Plaid (1992) and Hairpick (1994) — before he joined Poison as lead guitarist in 1993, replacing Richie Kotzen. He later pivoted toward production and film/TV composition, scoring work for numerous major productions. His early solo records sit in the instrumental rock-shred tradition with blues-influenced phrasing, making him a stylistic neighbor to the Texas blues-rock orbit. He is included here as a guitar voice in the broader context, not as a Double Trouble member.

## Chris Layton

Chris Layton is an American drummer who co-founded Double Trouble with bassist Tommy Shannon and Stevie Ray Vaughan in the late 1970s. He played on all four Double Trouble studio albums — Texas Flood, Couldn't Stand the Weather, Soul to Soul, and In Step — and his playing is a masterclass in restraint and dynamic control within a power trio format. After Vaughan's death in 1990, Layton and Shannon formed the Arc Angels with guitarists Doyle Bramhall II and Charlie Sexton. He has since worked with the Kenny Wayne Shepherd Band and various Texas blues projects. His shuffle feel and ability to respond to Vaughan's dynamic shifts in real time are central to the Double Trouble sound.

## Reese Wynans

Reese Wynans is an American keyboardist who joined Double Trouble in 1985 for the recording of Soul to Soul, bringing Hammond organ, piano, and synthesizer color to the previously guitar-bass-drums trio format. He remained with the band through In Step (1989) and until Vaughan's death in 1990. His organ work on "Life Without You" and his co-write credit on "Crossfire" (which won the Austin Chronicle Best Song award in 1990) are among the high points of his tenure. After SRV's death, Wynans worked extensively as a session and touring musician, eventually recording Sweet Release (2017) as a solo album with help from Joe Bonamassa and other blues figures.
