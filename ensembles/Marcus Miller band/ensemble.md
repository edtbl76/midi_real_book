# Ensemble: Marcus Miller band

## Context

Marcus Miller's band is a showcase for electric bass as a compositional instrument: Miller writes, produces, and plays nearly every part on studio records, then translates that vision to a touring group built around his longtime rhythm section. The group sits at the intersection of funk, jazz fusion, and R&B, drawing directly from Miller's decade-long production partnership with Miles Davis and David Sanborn. Poogie Bell's deep-pocket drumming, Hiram Bullock's blues-drenched session guitar, and Sanborn's instantly recognizable alto voice give the live band a rawer, more spontaneous quality than the tightly layered studio recordings.

## Marcus Miller

Marcus Miller is one of the most important electric bassists since Jaco Pastorius, combining elite slap-pop technique with an extraordinary melodic sense. He became a first-call NYC session bassist at 18, produced Miles Davis's late-career masterpieces "Tutu" (1986) and "Amandla" (1989), and sustained a long collaborative relationship with David Sanborn before building a substantial solo career spanning "The Sun Don't Lie" (1993) through "Afrodeezia" (2015).

## Hiram Bullock

Hiram Bullock (1955–2008) was a New York session guitarist whose combination of blues fire, jazz facility, and funk precision made him one of the most in-demand sidemen in the city. He was a founding member of the Late Night with David Letterman house band, a regular in David Sanborn's group, and a key collaborator on Marcus Miller's "The Sun Don't Lie" (1993).

## Poogie Bell

Poogie Bell is Marcus Miller's longtime touring drummer, providing the deep-pocket funk foundation that underpins Miller's live performances. He has appeared on multiple Miller studio albums and anchors the rhythm section that transforms Miller's layered studio productions into live ensemble music.

## Herbie Hancock

Herbie Hancock is one of the central figures in jazz since the 1960s, moving from the Miles Davis Quintet through acoustic post-bop, electric funk-fusion with the Headhunters, and commercial crossover work into the 1980s and beyond. His keyboard vocabulary — spanning acoustic piano, Rhodes, and synthesizer — is foundational to the jazz-fusion style that Marcus Miller and his band operate within.

| 1973–1975 | The Headhunters | [Wikipedia][headhunters-wiki] | — | Led the electric funk-fusion group that recorded "Head Hunters" and "Thrust." |
| 1978 | Solo (Sunlight) | [Wikipedia][herbie-hancock-wiki] | — | Explored vocoders and synthesized textures on "Sunlight." |

## David Sanborn

David Sanborn (1945–2024) was an alto saxophonist with one of the most instantly recognizable sounds in contemporary jazz and R&B — a blues-drenched, emotionally direct tone that made him a first-call session player for artists from Paul Simon to David Bowie, and a long-standing collaborator with Marcus Miller. He died in May 2024.
