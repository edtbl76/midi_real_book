# Ensemble: Funk Metal - Bettencourt

## Context

Extreme formed in Boston in 1985 around the core quartet of Gary Cherone (vocals), Nuno Bettencourt (guitar), Pat Badger (bass), and Paul Geary (drums, later replaced by Kevin Figueiredo). Their catalog spans a wide tonal range: the debut leans toward funk-inflected hard rock, Pornograffitti (1990) blends arena metal with acoustic balladry and earned them mainstream recognition, and III Sides to Every Story (1992) stretches into progressive and orchestral territory. What sets Nuno Bettencourt apart from standard hard rock guitarists is a percussive right-hand muting and tapping technique rooted in influences from Prince and Hendrix as much as Van Halen, paired with harmonic sophistication — extended voicings, jazz-adjacent chord choices — that gives even the heaviest Extreme tracks an unusually layered guitar voice.

## Dug Pinnick

Dug Pinnick is the bassist and co-vocalist of King's X, a band whose dense three-part harmonies and heavy groove-centered sound placed them at the intersection of hard rock, progressive metal, and funk throughout the late 1980s and 1990s. His bass playing is defined by a thick, chorused low-mid tone — often run through two rigs simultaneously — that gives King's X its characteristically massive, almost orchestral bottom end. Pinnick is a melodic bassist first: his lines move with the vocal and the harmony rather than simply anchoring the root, and he frequently doubles or counterpoints what Ty Tabor is playing on guitar. Beyond King's X he has pursued an extensive solo catalog (Poundhound, KXM) that deepens his exploration of heavy gospel-influenced rock. His approach rewards close listening to how a bassist can function as a harmonic and melodic voice, not just a rhythmic foundation.

## Nuno Bettencourt

Nuno Bettencourt is the guitarist of Extreme and one of the most technically distinctive hard rock players of the 1990s, distinguished by a right-hand percussive muting and tapping technique that gives his rhythm playing a percussive, almost keyboard-like attack. His influences run from Prince and Jimi Hendrix to Eddie Van Halen, but the synthesis is entirely his own: funk-derived rhythmic precision, harmonically sophisticated chord voicings that go well beyond pentatonic-anchored rock, and lead lines that combine legato fluency with aggressive picking dynamics. He has recorded six studio albums with Extreme (including their 2023 return *Six*), led the side project Mourning Widows, released a solo EP (*Schizophonic*, 1996), and performed as guitarist on Rihanna's world tours. His signature guitar, the Washburn N4, is a solidbody instrument with P-90-style pickups and a light, resonant construction that contributes to his articulate, less compressed tone.

## Jerry Gaskill

Jerry Gaskill is the drummer of King's X and one of the most musical, dynamically sensitive drummers in hard rock — a player who treats the drum kit as a tool for shaping the song's feel and emotional weight rather than simply marking time. His playing is rooted in deep pocket and groove, and he resists flashiness in favor of supporting King's X's dense vocal harmonies and bass-forward arrangements. Gaskill's cymbal work is particularly notable: his hi-hat and ride choices set the mood of a track as much as any note played by Tabor or Pinnick. He has recorded all of King's X's studio albums and has faced serious health challenges (including a heart attack in 2012 and a serious illness in 2018) while continuing to perform and contribute to the band. His restrained, song-serving approach is a masterclass in why drum parts that serve the music are more valuable than impressive displays of technique.
