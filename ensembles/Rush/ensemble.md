# Ensemble: Rush

## Context

Rush is a Canadian power trio formed in Toronto in 1968, arriving at its classic lineup when Neil Peart replaced drummer John Rutsey in 1974. What distinguishes them is the extreme technical and compositional demand placed on three players: Geddy Lee simultaneously manages bass, lead vocals, and a sprawling keyboard rig; Alex Lifeson builds complex prog-rock arrangements from unconventional chord voicings and layered effects; and Peart anchors it all with a kit that expanded to include concert toms, orchestral percussion, and electronic pads. The band evolved across five decades from hard rock rooted in Led Zeppelin and Cream, through the extended prog suites of the late 1970s, into the synth-driven sound of the 1980s before returning to a more guitar-forward approach in the 1990s and 2000s.

## Geddy Lee

Geddy Lee (born Gary Lee Weinrib, July 29, 1953, in Toronto) is the bassist, lead vocalist, and keyboardist for Rush, and alongside Alex Lifeson one of only two members to appear on every Rush album. His parents were Jewish survivors of the Nazi concentration camps Dachau and Bergen-Belsen; the name "Geddy" came from his mother's Polish-accented pronunciation of "Gary." Lee joined Rush in September 1968 at Lifeson's invitation, initially playing Fender Precision Bass before switching to a Rickenbacker 4001 in 1974, which defined the band's gritty mid-range tone through the late 1970s. From 1982 onward he incorporated the headless Steinberger L2 and then returned to a 1973 Fender Jazz Bass — later immortalized as the Fender Geddy Lee signature model — which he used for most recordings from Moving Pictures through the end of the band's run. He released one solo album, My Favourite Headache, in 2000, collaborating with guitarist Ben Mink and drummer Matt Cameron.

## Alex Lifeson

Alex Lifeson (born Aleksandar Živojinović, August 27, 1953, in Fernie, British Columbia) co-founded Rush in 1968 with drummer John Rutsey and is one of only two members to appear on every Rush album. He began playing guitar as a teenager in Toronto after receiving a classical acoustic guitar for Christmas, and first acquired a Gibson ES-335 in 1969, which became his primary instrument through the first four Rush studio albums. Lifeson is known for unorthodox chord voicings — often suspensions and open-string drones — and for navigating between acoustic delicacy and dense electric walls of sound within a single arrangement. Outside Rush, he released the solo album Victor (1996), a hard-alternative record with vocalist Edwin of I Mother Earth and bassist Les Claypool; after Peart's death in 2020, he formed Envy of None with vocalist Maiah Wynne and bassist Andy Curran, releasing two albums. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Rush in 2013 and made an Officer of the Order of Canada in 1996.

## Neil Peart

Neil Ellwood Peart (September 12, 1952 – January 7, 2020) was born in Hamilton, Ontario, and grew up in Port Dalhousie (now part of St. Catharines). He joined Rush in July 1974, replacing founding drummer John Rutsey, and quickly established himself as both the band's primary lyricist and one of rock's most technically demanding drummers. Peart was inducted into the Modern Drummer Readers Poll Hall of Fame in 1983 at age 30 — the youngest person ever so honored — and his live setup expanded through the decades to encompass concert toms, orchestral percussion (tympani, tubular bells, temple blocks, glockenspiel), and eventually a full electronic pad array integrated into his acoustic kit. Outside Rush, he produced two Buddy Rich tribute albums — Burning for Buddy (1994) and Burning for Buddy Vol. 2 (1997) — played on Jeff Berlin's Champion (1985) and Vertical Horizon's Burning the Days (2009), and wrote seven non-fiction books about his experiences as a motorcyclist and traveler. He died on January 7, 2020, of glioblastoma.
