# Ensemble: AC/DC

## Context

AC/DC formed in Sydney, Australia in 1973, founded by Scottish-born brothers Malcolm and Angus Young, and became one of the defining forces in hard rock through a sound built on economy and power rather than complexity. The band operated in two distinct eras: the Bon Scott years (1974–1980), marked by Scott's larrikin swagger and blues rawness, and the Brian Johnson era (1980–present), which opened with *Back in Black* and expanded the band's global reach. What makes AC/DC distinctive as an ensemble is the interlocking foundation of Malcolm Young's clean, dead-on rhythm guitar and Phil Rudd's metronomic, minimalist drumming — a locked pocket that leaves enormous headroom above it — against which Angus Young drives pentatonic blues-rock leads that are melodically direct, physically aggressive, and immediately recognisable.

## Cliff Williams

Cliff Williams was born in Romford, England in 1949 and came up playing in British rock bands before joining AC/DC in 1977, replacing Mark Evans on bass. He was recruited after a brief audition and quickly became a permanent fixture, contributing to every major AC/DC album from *Powerage* through *Rock or Bust*. His playing style is defined by simplicity and reliability: he locks tightly with Phil Rudd's kick drum, rarely embellishes, and keeps the low end clear and authoritative. Williams announced his retirement from touring in 2016 following Malcolm Young's inability to continue performing, though he returned for the *Power Up* album and 2024 world tour. His tone — a round, punchy Fender Precision sound — is a foundational element of the AC/DC sonic signature.

## Angus Young

Angus Young was born in Glasgow, Scotland in 1955 and emigrated with his family to Sydney as a child, where he began playing guitar seriously in his early teens. He co-founded AC/DC with his brother Malcolm in 1973 and has been the band's lead guitarist and primary visual identity throughout its entire history — known for performing in a schoolboy uniform and for physically kinetic live performances. His lead style draws almost exclusively from the blues pentatonic scale, favouring bends, vibrato, and raw tonal attack over technical complexity; he cites Chuck Berry and B.B. King as primary influences. Angus uses no effects pedals, achieving his tone entirely through guitar-into-amplifier, and his setup has remained essentially unchanged for over fifty years. He is the last remaining founding member actively leading the band, and produced AC/DC's *Power Up* (2020) following Malcolm's death in 2017.

## Malcolm Young

Malcolm Young was born in Glasgow, Scotland in 1953, the elder brother of Angus, and co-founded AC/DC in Sydney in 1973 after the family emigrated. He was the band's primary rhythm guitarist, chief songwriter, and de facto musical director throughout his career — the strategic and sonic anchor around whom every other element was organised. His playing style is defined by absolute economy: a clean, dry rhythm tone using a Gretsch Jet Firebird with the middle pickup removed, played through Marshall amplification without effects, hitting chord voicings with a physical authority that few rhythm guitarists have matched. Malcolm was diagnosed with dementia in 2014 and quietly stepped back from the band before his condition was made public; he died in November 2017 at age 64. His contribution to the architecture of hard rock rhythm guitar is widely considered foundational.

## Phil Rudd

Phil Rudd was born Philip Hugh Norman Witschke Rudzevecius in Melbourne, Australia in 1954 and joined AC/DC in 1975, playing on all of the band's most celebrated albums through *Flick of the Switch* (1983) before departing under difficult circumstances. He was replaced by Simon Wright and then Chris Slade before returning to the band in 1994 for *Ballbreaker*, remaining through *Rock or Bust* (2014). His drumming style is one of the most distinctive in rock: an almost mechanically precise four-on-the-floor approach with minimal fills, extreme restraint on the hi-hat, and a weight-forward kick pattern that locks the entire band into a single rhythmic intention. He uses no triggers or electronic augmentation. Rudd's playing is a defining reason why AC/DC records feel physically powerful at any volume — the locked pocket he and Malcolm Young created together is the engine the guitar and vocal ride on.

## Bon Scott

Bon Scott was born Ronald Belford Scott in Kirriemuir, Scotland in 1946 and emigrated with his family to Australia as a child, growing up in Fremantle, Western Australia. He joined AC/DC in 1974 as lead vocalist, replacing Dave Evans, after already having experience as the drummer and vocalist for The Valentines and Fraternity. Scott was the band's lyrical identity during its most musically formative period — his combination of larrikin humour, genuine menace, and raw blues feeling gave early AC/DC a personality that distinguished it sharply from its peers. He co-wrote the band's early material with Malcolm and Angus Young and was a committed musical partner, not merely a frontman. Scott died in London in February 1980 at age 33 following a night of heavy drinking; his death came just as AC/DC had broken internationally on the strength of *Highway to Hell*.

## Brian Johnson

Brian Johnson was born in Dunston, Gateshead, England in 1947 and came to AC/DC's attention after the Young brothers saw him perform with his band Geordie in the 1970s, and remembered him when they needed to replace Bon Scott following Scott's death in February 1980. Johnson was selected after auditions held in London; his first recording with the band, *Back in Black* (1980), became one of the best-selling albums in history. His vocal style is characterised by a raspy, high-pitched scream and an approach to delivery that is physically extreme — Johnson has described his technique as essentially shouting in pitch — giving AC/DC's post-Scott material a different but equally potent character. He has collaborated on AC/DC's songwriting throughout his tenure and remained with the band through *Power Up* (2020) and the subsequent world tour, with a brief interruption in 2016 due to hearing difficulties that threatened his ability to perform. Johnson is considered one of hard rock's definitive vocalists.

## Michael Schenker

Michael Schenker was born January 10, 1955, in Sarstedt, West Germany. His elder brother Rudolf co-founded the Scorpions; Michael joined briefly for their debut *Lonesome Crow* (1972) before being recruited at age 17 by British hard rock band UFO, with whom he made his most celebrated recordings from 1973 to 1978 — covering *Phenomenon* through *Obsession*. His UFO work established him as one of hard rock's most distinctive lead voices: melodic, emotionally direct, technically precise, operating almost exclusively from a polished silver/black Gibson Flying V through Marshall amplification. After leaving UFO, he formed the Michael Schenker Group (MSG), releasing the self-titled debut (1980) and *MSG* (1981) among others, and has continued leading various MSG lineups including Michael Schenker Fest (2017 onward).

## Joe Perry

Joe Perry (born Anthony Joseph Perry, September 10, 1950, in Lawrence, Massachusetts) is the lead guitarist and principal riff architect of Aerosmith, whose blues-rooted playing style and raw, slightly dangerous tone gave the band its defining guitar identity. He co-founded Aerosmith with Steven Tyler in 1970 and wrote or co-wrote signature riffs on Walk This Way, Back in the Saddle, Sweet Emotion, and dozens more. Perry left Aerosmith in 1979 following escalating personal conflicts, formed the Joe Perry Project (*Let the Music Do the Talking*, 1980), and rejoined the classic lineup in 1984 for *Done with Mirrors*. His lead style emphasises blues-scale bends, open-string riffing, and a slightly overdriven tone that prioritises feel over technical precision; the blues-rock hard rock vocabulary he operates in is directly adjacent to AC/DC's.
